Two days of soft, buffering nonsense. Then the buffering stops.

Arenya is standing in her parents' backyard in Daly City, and the backyard has too many directions. She can see the fence from the front and from behind at the same time. She can see the curry-leaf tree from *inside* its own growing. Fog pours along the ground, and it isn't weather. It moves like a tide that has an opinion, thick and dark and patient, and it's breathing. At the bottom of the garden is Susmithaa Aunty's cottage, except the cottage window *is* the brass lamp, the kuthu vilakku. The lamp is the size of a temple, and every one of its wicks is burning.

Her aunt stands barefoot at the center of it, and she is not her aunt. Or she is, the way a sunrise is a light bulb. She's a woman made of lamps: a thousand small flames in the shape of a woman, braids of fire with tiny silver charms ringing in them. Far beneath her, the way you see the bottom of a pool, Arenya can make out a small woman in a cardigan standing in the real garden, perfectly still, staring at the street. Half a step behind the burning woman a lanky figure flickers in and out of the frame. He wears a shabby long coat, swings a light on a chain like a lantern, and swears steadily in something that might be cockney.

Four men stand around them. Arenya can't hold onto their faces. They slide off her attention like rain off glass, smudged thumbprints, each wearing a halo of grey static. Between the four of them a net is being drawn tight: humming filaments that *pull*, like magnets, dragging flame after flame toward the center and snuffing it. One of the men wears a light grey jacket. She's seen that jacket before. It was on her wall.

The flames are going out one by one. Susmithaa turns her head through an angle that isn't an angle and looks straight at Arenya.

"Kanna." It's the most frightened Arenya has ever heard her. "You came. Of course you came. *Listen to me.*"

"Aunty." It comes out small, like she's six years old and lost in the Serramonte mall. She swallows and tries again, sharper, the way she talks when she's scared. "Okay. I'm listening. I'm *listening*. Tell me what to do."

She doesn't wait for permission. She reaches out, the way you'd reach for someone who's slipping on a wet step, toward the nearest flame in her aunt's burning hand. While she does it she makes herself look at the man in the grey jacket. She doesn't look at his face, because her eyes keep skidding off it. She looks at the jacket. The collar, the seam at the shoulder, how it hangs. *Details. You can always hold onto details.* She tries to pin him the way she'd pin a still frame: *Tartine. Corner table. You.*

"I know you," she says to him, and her voice shakes. "I've got you on a wall."

"I pick," she says. "*The kid.* Always the kid, Abernathy. Stand there. Don't move from there."

At 3:20 her phone lights up on the nightstand. **Amma.**

And from the kitchen, through the half-open door, she hears her kettle being filled, and somebody swearing at it under his breath, in cockney.
