**Wednesday, November 4. 6:00 a.m.**

Every phone in the car buzzes at once. **6:22 a.m. Crissy Field.**

The Presidio is still blue-black. The bridge is a string of red lights hung in nothing, because the fog bank sits on the Gate like a held breath, low and exact, a quarter mile out and not moving. The promenade is wet. A Rivian stands alone in the warming-hut lot, ticking as it cools.

He's on the picnic deck, early, as promised. **Typhus Quinn** is taller than he looks on a screen and more tired. He wears a runner's shell over a collared shirt, as if he couldn't decide which man to be this morning. There are two paper coffees on the rail beside him, both large and both black, and a manila folder flattened under his left hand as though it might blow away. He isn't on his phone. He's watching the fog.

Out at the Gate, the foghorn sounds. It's a low two-note bellow. Then nothing. Then, eleven seconds later, it sounds again.

Anyone who stood at Candlestick on Monday knows that interval in their teeth.

A jogger in a black headband comes down the promenade from the east, passes the hut at an easy pace, and keeps going toward the bridge. Behind the hut on Mason Street, a white Sprinter van sits at the curb with its engine off. A small laminated sign in the windshield reads **GGNRA / FOG-SENSOR SURVEY**.

Typhus sees Arenya before she reaches the deck. He doesn't wave. He picks up one of the coffees and holds it out across the rail, the way you'd offer something to a person you weren't sure would take it.

"I didn't know how you take it," he says. "So I didn't do anything to it." The horn sounds. He waits it out, and something in his face says he's been counting too. "Thank you for coming. I'm going to ask you to tell me I'm wrong about something, and I'd like you to actually try."
