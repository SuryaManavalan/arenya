**6:22 a.m. Crissy Field.**

The Presidio is still blue-black. The bridge is a string of red lights hung in nothing, because the fog bank sits on the Gate like a held breath, low and exact, a quarter mile out and not moving. The promenade is wet. A Rivian stands alone in the warming-hut lot, ticking as it cools.

He's on the picnic deck, early, as promised. **Typhus Quinn** is taller than he looks on a screen and more tired. He wears a runner's shell over a collared shirt, as if he couldn't decide which man to be this morning. Out at the Gate, the foghorn sounds. It's a low two-note bellow. Then nothing. Then, eleven seconds later, it sounds again.

Anyone who stood at Candlestick on Monday knows that interval in their teeth.

A jogger in a black headband comes down the promenade from the east, passes the hut at an easy pace, and keeps going toward the bridge. Behind the hut on Mason Street, a white Sprinter van sits at the curb with its engine off. A small laminated sign in the windshield reads **GGNRA / FOG-SENSOR SURVEY**.
