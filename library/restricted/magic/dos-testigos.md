# Dos testigos

A ledger mark of el Contador's clerks: two short upright strokes side by side in the margin (beside a horizontal line). It means *this entry is contested and stays open until two stand for it.* When a body's name was disputed, the clerk did not cross it out; he drew the strokes. [#0202]

## Details
- Appeared in the salt beside Susmithaa's ruled line after her March no at the shed door. Nana first took it for wind; after the lamp split in two, she reads it as: the next *no* takes two people standing together. [#0198]
- Nana: it isn't foreknowledge, merely "a space" the clerk leaves on the line waiting for a signature. [#0202]
- Arenya said that if it comes to that, she'd want Kino as her second; Kino accepted in principle, "not tonight." Neither is committed. [#0200, #0201]
- A "second pen" pressure has appeared in Arenya's palm beside the cold 12. [#0202]

- b1.c15.s09: At the stone yard, row 12's polished margin stone bears a single pencil stroke, "Testigo: una. Falta una." Kino was asked *¿Y tú?* and declined; the clerk ticked Arenya as "referred" and left a pencil dot beside the rule under her 12. The second stroke is still unwritten. [#0226, #0229, #0232]

- b1.c16.s08: Fulfilled in Colma's book. Nana Itzel's pencil strokes beside her witness signature complete the pair; Arenya's palm: 12, rule, two graphite strokes. Nana: one stroke is a *no* that can be argued, two a *no* that stands; on the sixteenth day the account reads *disputada, dos testigos* and the clerk does not pay out on a dispute; Bell "can wait." The no does **not** reach the Livermore temple, a different book. Per Nana, the witness (not the signer) is kept in the book with their household. [#0474, #0484]

## Open questions
