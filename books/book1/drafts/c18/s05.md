The water was leaving.

Arenya watched it go from the riprap, the way you watch a guest decide to leave a party. By 7:40 the bay had drawn back past where it had any right to be, and what it left behind was a long black plain of mud with a skin of shine on it. The mud ticked and sucked like a kitchen settling at night.

One sodium lamp buzzed on its pole. The lot was empty.

Sixty yards out, the second piling stood in the dark, a tarred stump furred with mussels. Three iron stakes were driven around it in a triangle, exactly where they'd been on Monday. In the middle stood the survey rod, its orange paint gone brown.

Northeast, across the cove, the Hunters Point stack was darker than the sky behind it. It wasn't lit and it wasn't quiet. She felt it hum in her back teeth.

Then she let the Lamp open behind her eyes, and the triangle turned into something else.

A ruled line ran from each stake. Thin, blue-grey, like the lines on ledger paper, taut as piano wire. They ran *uphill* over the mud toward the stack, and the seawater slid along them in bright threads, the way rain follows a clothesline. The rod was where the three were tied off. A pen dot at the end of a sentence.

In Ava's pocket the kelp letter ticked once. *Tock.* Addressed to her alone.

"Pictures first," Arenya said. "Nobody touches anything until I've got it in the can."

She walked the mud with her phone up, flash off. The triangle wide, then each stake close with the rod in frame, then the stack behind it. Kino and Caleb followed in her prints. Ava stayed at the riprap where the lamp reached, phone propped on a rock, wrist up.

"Round one, 7:40," Ava called, flat as a case report. "Six minutes a round. That truck is my other job. If it moves, I say *truck* once, and you stop talking."

Across the cove, under a yard light, the box truck sat in its own yellow puddle. Not moving.

*Yet,* Arenya thought.

At the first stake she crouched. Two fingers beside the blue-grey line, not on it. She wasn't going to cut. She was going to coax, warm and slow, a thumb working a knot loose. The wire sagged into the seawater like a wet thread. Out in the shallows something old breathed out through a hundred clam-holes at once.

"Okay, ma'am," she said, to the tide-woman, wherever she was. "With manners."

Kino knelt and wrapped both hands low on the iron. "*Perdón, señora. Sólo prestado un momento.*" A quarter turn and back. Ease it out of the mud's fist. His forearm woke with the cold nib-scratch, and he let it itch.

It gave. It gave an inch too little.

Caleb's hands closed under his. "On the beat," he said. "The tide's beat." They lifted, and the stake came up with a long, wet *suck* that bounced off the riprap. Eighteen inches of black square-section iron, stamped near the head with a small ruled box. The kind you'd tick.

Caleb's palms went white where they'd touched it.

And the rod went quiet.

The hum didn't fade. It *stopped*, like a reader looking up from the page. The two remaining lines went taut and still. Across the cove, the white roll-up door rattled up a foot, and yellow light lay flat on the asphalt beneath it.

Nobody got out.

"Door's up one foot, 7:46," Ava said, very evenly. "Same tell as Monday. It opens right before it hums." A breath. "My hands are shaking. Noting that for the record. Not stopping."

* * *

Abernathy had gone out ahead of her, as he'd said he would. He stood where the three lines met, coat not moving with the wind, one brass-buttoned cuff laid against the second line and a clerk's flat palm held over it, the way you'd hold a hand over a till.

*If it goes quiet, that's when you worry,* he'd said.

*It's gone quiet,* she thought at him. *Abernathy.*

*I know, love.* His voice on the bond was thin and quick. *Wrong page, ma'am. Nothing here.* His chin was down, his face dull, a clerk nobody remembers. He drew the cuff across the line in one clean stroke, the way you strike a figure from a column.

It missed by a hair.

The line turned the page back.

She saw it only through the bond. A column of figures opened in the air beside him, in an old copperplate hand, a heading with its top line smudged. A date column ran down past where she could read. Under the heading there was a single legible word.

**Porter.**

The line leaned toward him like a pen nib finding its place. His collar cinched. Cold ran up the cuff into his wrist, ink-cold, filing-cabinet cold, and he made a sound she'd never heard from him. It had no cockney in it at all.

She was already crossing the shine at a crouch-run.

"Hold," she said. "Don't let go of the cuff, I've got you." She laid her left palm flat on the line just above his brass and poured the Lamp down it. Not cutting. A hand under his elbow. "Wrong page. Wrong man. Strike it, clerk."

Across the cove a figure ducked under the door.

Grey steel-toes. Hi-vis with no logo. He stood on the yellow asphalt with a long handheld light, not a flashlight but something with a white door's hum in it, and he pointed it at the water. The beam didn't touch the mud. It lit the *lines*. It started walking down the second one toward them, slow as a finger down a page.

"Worker out!" Ava said. "Grey steel-toes, 7:48. His light walks the *lines*, not the mud. One round from the piling, maybe less. Nobody stands. Nobody silhouettes."

Arenya looked at Abernathy's face, what there was of it. The page beside him was still open. He wasn't looking at the figures. He was looking at the one word like it was written on the inside of his eyelid.

"Let go," he said, out loud, to nobody, and then on the bond, rough: *Don't, love. I'll not stand to be read by a stranger's lamp.*

*Abernathy.*

*That page had a name on it and it wasn't one I gave you.*

She understood, then, and hated it. She nodded.

He unbuttoned the brass cuff with his free hand. He slid his wrist out of it and stepped back, and the cuff stayed on the line like a coat left on a hook. He went grey and flat and stood behind the piling, where the beam could find no edge to catch on.

*Porter,* she sent after him. *That's what it says. I saw it. I'm not asking now.*

*After,* he said. *Not on the mud.*

* * *

Kino and Caleb had taken the second stake without waiting for the light. "Now," Kino said. "Lift on the beat. Don't let it read your hands." Quarter turn, then up, steady, the way you'd draw a root without tearing the bank.

It came out clean. A small *pock*, and the mud closed.

With its iron gone, the second line stopped reading. It went slack all at once, and Arenya's palm was still on it. For one breath the Lamp ran the whole length of the thread unopposed, lit gold across the cove, and the page beside the piling *shut*. The copperplate folded away into nothing.

Caleb had the iron against his chest under the grey jacket before it could throw a shine. The two of them went flat in the shadow.

But the empty cuff was still hanging on the line, and the line was going home.

It slithered off the mud toward the water and the stack, reeling itself in, and the brass rode it, bumping along the shine an arm's length past her fingers. Then two.

She let it go.

*A cuff is a cuff,* she told herself, and it was a lie, and she knew it. *A stake is the whole point.*

Across the cove, the beam ran out to the end of a line that wasn't there anymore. It stopped on the water ten yards short of the piling and held. The man lifted a radio. His voice carried over the slack water, bored and precise.

"*Two's gone soft. Logging it.*"

Arenya took the third stake. It came up like a tooth with nothing left holding it. She shot the hole first, then the hole again with her glove beside it for scale, then hugged the iron under her blazer with her back to the stack.

"That's three," she whispered. "Abernathy, I'm sorry about the cuff. We're coming back for it. I'm not saying that to be nice."

*Leave the rod,* he said, thin and dry. *You said coming back, and I'll hold you to it, but not tonight. It's the pen dot. Touch it and they'll feel it in the stack.* A pause. *I'm sat behind a piling being a very dull wall, thank you for asking.*

Ava was ticking it off on her fingers. "Leave the rod. He's logging, not hunting, and I'd rather he log *soft* than *intruders*. We go at 7:51, with a minute to spare. Nobody runs. Running makes noise, and he's got a radio."

Nobody ran.

They went out low along the riprap, jackets zipped over cold stamped steel, Abernathy last. At the water's edge Kino touched two fingers to the wet. "*Gracias, abuela,*" he said, barely aloud. "*Tenemos tres. Falta lo demás.*"

*We have three. The rest is still owed.*

At 7:51 by Ava's thumb, the man under the sodium light lowered his lamp, said something short into the radio, and ducked back inside. The white door rattled down. The yellow strip narrowed to a line and went out.

Behind them the rod stood alone in the incoming tide, humming to itself, reading one line now instead of three.

Under Kino's fingers the water moved *against* the current, once, a slow push, like a hand closing over a hand. Out on the flats something old settled lower into the bay with a sound like a long exhale, and the reek of rust that had hung over the piling all evening thinned until it was only salt.

Not free. *Looser.*

* * *

At the car, with the doors shut and the heater on and Caleb holding his burned hands palms-up in his lap like something he'd been handed, nobody talked.

Arenya watched the windshield fog. She was thinking of one word in an old hand, and of the way a man she'd never once seen frightened had said *yes* before he could say *no.*

In Ava's pocket, the kelp letter ticked.

Once. Twice. Three times.

A fourth.
