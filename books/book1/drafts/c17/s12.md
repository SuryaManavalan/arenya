By nine-forty the house had emptied toward its edges.

Appa and Amma sat in the front room with the television off and the blinds half down, not talking. Ava was on the porch with her phone to her ear, reading numbers to someone. Caleb was still at the table because he'd been told to sit there, and he hadn't asked if he could get up. Nana had gone to lie down. *Ten minutes, not one more.*

The kitchen belonged to Kino.

The comal was cold. The salt under its cloth looked like a sleeping animal. The window over the sink was still fogged from Ava's coffee, and in the middle of the glass, at the height of a seated man's eyes, was the circle. One palm wide. Wiped clean from the inside.

Too low for anyone standing at the sink.

Someone had wiped it sitting down. Or kneeling.

Arenya stopped beside Kino and didn't touch the glass. "One condition," she said, low. "You sit it, you don't talk to it. If it writes back, or the circle gets bigger, you stand up and walk away. I don't care how polite it is."

Behind them the pantry door stood open a finger's width. Nobody remembered leaving it that way. She pushed it shut with two fingers.

"Abernathy. You're with me. Not by the window."

"I'll be in the next room with the door open," she told Kino, quieter. "I'm not leaving. I'm just not stupid."

Then she dragged a chair out from the table and set it three feet behind him, facing his back. The oven door was dark and polished enough to show the whole window, reversed. If the circle changed, she'd see it first.

*Sit by me,* she thought, toward the bond. *Not the pantry. And when his hour's up, you owe me the patient voice. All of it. Who knew your name before you gave it?*

*Right. Beside you, love,* said Abernathy, at her knee, in the accent he wore like a borrowed hat. *Not the pantry. The pantry was a tactical retreat, and the tactics were mostly my knees, which I haven't got.*

Then the hat slipped a half-inch.

*It was in the walled-off place. When the runner stood on the step and Dalal's glasses went down on the wood. Patient. Not unkind. Like someone who's got all day, because they have. And it said a short name at me. Not Abernathy. Shorter. One breath of it. I knew it the way you know your own cough.*

*I didn't answer. I didn't decide not to, mind. I just didn't, and it let me not, and that's the part that frightens me. Anything that wants something pushes. This waited.*

*Whose is it?*

*I don't know, guv. I won't pretend. Watch the oven door.*

* * *

Kino sent Caleb out first.

"You can get up," he said, without turning. "Front room, or the porch with Ava. Not the floor." It wasn't a verdict. It was what Appa had given him, a place to be. Caleb went, and the screen door sighed shut behind him.

Kino didn't touch the pantry. He took a pinch of salt from the jar and set it on the linoleum in front of the gap, one small hill. Not a wall. The kind of thing you leave on a step for someone, so they know you saw them.

Then he laid the rest along the sill, corner to corner, slowly, and didn't let the line break, not even where the sill dipped by the faucet. His hands were shaking a little. He let them.

He pulled one of Nana's chairs square to the window, knees against the cabinet, and sat. The circle sat right at his eyes. Whoever wiped it had sat about where he sat. He rested his hands flat on his thighs, the left forearm turned up so the thumbprint showed, and breathed the way he'd been taught. Four in. Hold. Four out. Slow enough that the room got louder.

The refrigerator. Ava's voice through the porch door. The clock over the stove.

"Buenos días," he said, the way you ask on the mountain at night. "Soy Joaquín, nieto de Itzel. This is her house. You wiped the glass to look out, or to look in. I'm not going to chase you. I'm going to sit here an hour. If you want to be seen, I'm the one who's looking."

He kept his eyes on the clean circle, and did not turn toward the pantry.

* * *

The hour did what hours do.

At nine fifty-two there was nothing. The hydrant. The grey crust of salt Kino had laid on the curb, scuffed by a jogger. A neighbor's Halloween skeleton slumped sideways on its porch rail. Past the rooftops, the fog lay on the water, flat and quiet. It wasn't breathing. Ava laughed once on the porch, short, at something Caleb hadn't meant to be funny.

At ten-oh-seven the steam had dried everywhere but the edge of the circle. There it stayed beaded, as if something on the other side were breathing on it.

Arenya didn't blink much. She watched the oven door.

At ten twenty-one the clock over the stove stopped.

Kino counted it without meaning to. Eleven seconds. Then the tick came back, like nothing had happened.

Out past the roofs, the fog bank lifted and settled, lifted and settled. On the third breath it didn't come all the way down. A long low line stayed above the water a moment too long, the way a back stays above the surface when something rolls.

Then it was fog again.

In the oven door, Arenya saw the circle reversed. And at the center of it, a smudge. On the *outside* of the glass. About the height of a forehead, as if someone had leaned in to look.

She could see the step. There was no one on it.

*Kino,* she did not say. Her mouth wouldn't. She kept her hands in her lap.

A fingertip drew on the glass from outside. Slowly, through the beads. One flat horizontal stroke: a waterline. Then a low hump, rising out of it.

Then it stopped.

Unfinished, like the fifth stroke on the Nissan's window. It wrote no word. It wrote no name. The beads ran, and the drawing ran with them.

Nothing spoke.

At her knee, Abernathy had gone as still as he could, which for him was a flicker held at the edges. *Closer,* he said, hardly a voice at all. *The blank page. Like a held breath on the other side of a door. It's not cold, love. It's like being waited on.*

He didn't move toward it. He didn't move away.

* * *

At ten-forty Kino turned his chair.

The salt on the sill was unbroken from corner to corner. The dip by the faucet had held. But the little hill on the linoleum in front of the pantry was gone. Not scattered. Gone. The floor was clean, as if someone had picked it up the way you pick up a thing left on a step for you.

Abernathy's edges stuttered. For a frame he was in two places, then one. Nothing had touched him. It never did.

*Manners, love,* he said. *Not a debt. A debt would've asked.* A pause. *I don't know why I'm sure. I'm sure, and I don't like it.*

Kino touched the clean place with two fingers, flat. Nothing. Cold, maybe.

"It drew the waterline," he said, to Arenya, to the oven door. "The back coming up. Then it stopped, same as the Nissan. It didn't write a name. It didn't come in. It took the salt like a gift off a step." He flexed his hands. They'd stopped shaking. "It's not hunting us. It's showing us something and can't finish."

"I saw it in the reflection," Arenya said. "A forehead, outside the glass, leaning in. Then a finger. I could see the step the whole time." She opened the notebook at last. *10:21. Clock stopped 11 sec. Smudge, forehead height, outside. Waterline. Hump. Unfinished.* She wrote nothing else.

"Don't wipe the glass," Kino said. "Photos before it dries. Circle, sill, stove clock."

She took them. Click, click, click. Eleven seconds, frozen on the stove.

"The salt's the part I don't like," she said. "Somebody took the gift."

"Nana'll know if that's manners or a debt."

"And the pantry stays shut." She put the notebook away. "Then we tell Malachi about the blank page. He said he heard it too."

*That drawing, guv,* Abernathy said, once Kino had turned back to the sill. *The waterline and the hump. I've seen that stroke before, and I couldn't tell you where. It's not a thought. It's a flinch. Write that down too, and don't ask me for more. I'd only be guessing, and I won't lie to you.*

She wrote it down.
