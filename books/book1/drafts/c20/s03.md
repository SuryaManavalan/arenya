# Only Yours

Caleb's counter reaches the water a heartbeat too late.

The grey shoulder folds under the surf and doesn't come back up. The eight kelp strands lie on the wet sand with their ends open, like fingers somebody forgot to close. Then the seventh one lifts, slides across the sand, and settles over Caleb's boot like a ribbon someone's laid down on purpose.

I don't hear it with my ears. It comes up through the soles of my shoes, a voice on a bad line.

*In your ear, before the breath. Only yours.*

Caleb goes the colour of the foam.

I step past Malachi's shoulder and crouch beside him, close enough that the notebook shields my mouth from the others. I don't touch him. His hands are out of his pockets and I can see the tremor from here, fine and fast, like a wire in the wind.

"Caleb. Look at me, not the water." I keep my voice flat, the way I'd talk a source off a ledge. "Word for word. Don't make it nicer."

He tells me. I write the time at the top of a clean page, *7:12*, and under it, *Sunday dusk. Only yours. Sender agrees.* I underline *sender* once. The seventh strand hasn't moved since it let go of nothing.

* * *

Malachi keeps his hands in his coat. He doesn't raise his voice. A man who raises his voice has already lost the room, and he has never once looked like he was losing anything.

"Nobody volunteers blind," he says to the water. He's looking at the place in the sand where the shoulder went under, not at the strands. "A box moves Sunday at 11:40. A holder pays for it. So here's the question. Who benefits? And does that person know the price is being paid in somebody else's name?"

He takes one step toward the tide line. Not a threat. Close enough to be heard over the surf.

"If the answer is the Chair, this is over before anyone breathes. I'm not holding a door open for the thing that asked for Pryce's seat at three in the morning."

He doesn't look at Caleb when he's finished. He gives him the silence he's earned, and then he waits, the way he waits for everything.

* * *

The eighth strand answers.

It lifts out of the foam and points at Malachi. Between one wave and the next, the wet sand beside it darkens, as if a finger were pressing down from underneath:

*THE BENEFIT IS NOT THE CHAIR'S. THE NAME GOES TO THE MOUTH, NOT TO THE CHAIR. THE KEEPER LOOKING AWAY IS THE PRICE WE ASK THE CHAIR TO PAY.*

The next wave takes the words back.

When Ava's pen scratches *who keeps the pump house clock*, the foam hisses once, and a voice that isn't quite a voice says from nowhere in particular, "Not ours. We only hear it. It runs faster than the tide."

I copy the terms while I still have them. *One breath. Holder's pulse drops. Shell-coloured lips. Own name lost a dozen hours. Sunday 11:40. Choose by Sunday dusk.* Under *sender* I write *the one the creek runs to*, and then the pen stops moving on its own, and I let it.

Up the dune road, Ollie's head comes up.

He's on a driftwood log with his knees pulled to his chest, wrapped in Kino's hoodie, and the Crown at his temple has the dull shine it had at three in the morning. His lips move. No breath goes with the words, and I only catch them because I'm looking at him.

"*Sunday. Bring someone who can sign.*"

Then he blinks, flinches, and says in his own cracked voice, "Sorry. What did I say? Did I say something?"

My hand goes to the back pocket of my jeans, where the receipt is folded under the stub of Nana's pestle. *ALTERNATE SIGNATORY: L. SOWRI ✓.* I don't take it out. I don't say the name. Under the copied terms I write one line, small enough that nobody reading over my shoulder gets it:

*Who is "someone who can sign"? Check against the receipt.*

* * *

Kino doesn't go to the water. He goes up the dune road to Ollie, because Ollie is the one who can't stand still, and Kino is the one who gave him the hoodie.

He crouches in front of the log and puts two fingers on the kid's wrist. Thin. Fast. Cold.

"Four in. Six out," he says. "Count with me." Ollie does it badly. Kino keeps counting until Ollie is doing it alone.

Then he asks the question Arenya sent up the path, the way he'd ask a child where he last saw his shoe. "When you said *someone who can sign*, what were you looking at? Not the Chair. Not the water. What was in front of you?"

Ollie thinks for a long time. "The car," he says. "The beige one, down the far end of the lot. Or I thought it was. The window was down and somebody was reading a receipt out loud." He shakes his head hard. "I don't know who. I don't know if I heard it or if the Crown made it up. Don't write that I'm sure."

Kino looks down the road. The lot is empty except for Ava's Subaru and a gull walking the painted line. Nobody is standing closer to Ollie than he is.

He leaves the kid where he is, and he walks back down the path with his hands loose at his sides.

* * *

Caleb's pulse is the only thing I can hear.

Ava has his right wrist in her fingers, his palm turned up to the sky. She isn't being gentle about it. She's being fast. "One eighteen," she says, counting against the cheap watch on her own arm. "Going down. Hold still. Look at my pen, not the water."

*7:14 a.m. Caleb, 116. Palms cold. No blue at the nail beds. Tremor fine-motor. Not dose-shaped.*

She writes it while she counts, time first, because time is the one thing she trusts out here. Then, underneath, in a smaller hand: *Clock runs faster than the tide. Faster, or just ahead of it?*

"Ask it that," she says to the foam, as if it were a patient. "Ahead of it, or faster. Those mean different things to a person with a stopwatch." Then, lower, to Caleb: "And nobody here is answering for you. You're counting with me."

He pulls his left pocket inside out, slowly, so nobody can read it as a grab. The dropper comes out still capped. He holds it up where Ava can see.

"This is Vesper. I'm not taking it. I'm saying that out loud, so it counts." He puts it in her palm and closes her fingers over it with his own, which is a stupid thing to do with shaking hands, and he does it anyway. "Keep it. Give it back Sunday. Or never."

He looks at the seventh strand, which is still lying across his boot, and then at Malachi.

"Nobody signs anything at 118. I'm not answering the water until I've slept, and I haven't slept since Tuesday." He breathes out longer than he breathes in. "And *sign* goes on the list with *Chair*. I don't trust either one from anything that talks up through a heel."

Ava's question goes out to the surf, and the answer comes back up through the sand at once, a low pressure under all our feet: "Ahead. It is always ahead. We only hear where it will be."

Caleb's count drops to 104 under her fingers. The seventh strand slides off his boot like a ribbon let go, and the eighth follows it into the foam. The wave that takes them leaves nothing written.

In my notebook, beside *sender*, a new word appears. I didn't write it. My hand is still.

*Listening.*

Nobody has signed anything. It's 7:19 on Saturday morning, and Sunday dusk is thirty-some hours off. Typhus calls at seven tonight.
