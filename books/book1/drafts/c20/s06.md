The ink was still wet.

Arenya held her phone low, flashlight tilted away so the beam wouldn't sit on the page, and photographed the new line under *Alternate accepted*. Then the hinge of the lid. Then the frost line on the bumper, where the cuff in L-3 was sweating cold into the steel.

She read the line twice under her breath. Once more out loud, slowly, so nobody could say she'd skipped a word.

"Who sits is not who signs. Who signs is not who is asked." She looked at Malachi, not at the chair. "That's a rule about the appointment. Not a threat."

*Quiet, guv.* Abernathy, right against the back of her neck, where no one else could hear. *That cuff's warm enough to fry an egg. Don't touch the lid. And don't you dare say your name near it, not even under your breath.*

"Nobody moves L-3," she said to the dark, "until we know what's in it. And nobody says their own name near that lid."

Out past the chain-link, between two rusted rail ties, Kino said something low about a dream and the Lady, a rule he'd heard in the same shape and not the same words. *Whoever wrote that line knew the one who's asked is the one left holding the pen.* He didn't come closer. He looked at the chair the way he'd look at a rattlesnake on a trail. It wasn't doing anything, so he wouldn't either.

* * *

In the car, Ava had Caleb's wrist.

Ninety-one, and he was counting in threes again. The pen note under *Alternate accepted* was a slant she knew. She'd seen it in Arenya's notebook, in the two lines Arenya hadn't written. She didn't say so. She wrote *00:09, same slant. Not sure. Check the loops.*

Caleb let her see his hands shake. That was new. "The first letter made my hand jump," he said, low, like a man talking in a room with a sleeping person. "Don't count it as a name. It's the sound the Crown makes when it wants a hand on something. The fifth isn't mine." He looked at his knees. "You'll get the plain version. Not tonight. Tonight I'd tell it wrong and you'd write it down wrong."

Malachi wrote 00:07 in his own notebook, and a line under it. *Nobody signs.* He kept his hands out of his coat where anyone on the spur could see them, and he did not look at the chair again.

* * *

12:14. The truck's cab radio clicked on by itself.

A dispatcher's flat voice, the bay's hiss behind it. "D2-04, M-Ops line. Confirm seated." No name. It waited the way a clerk waits, pen up.

Under *Alternate accepted* the wet ink moved. The slant drew itself, loop by loop.

**Seated by proxy at 12:20 if refused by no one aloud.**

L-3's latch gave one click. The chair didn't move, but the pale hollow in its seat began to look *used*. In the back seat Caleb's tremor climbed into his shoulders, and the Crown's sound went up a half tone, and every head in the car turned toward the open door, and nobody had been asked anything yet.

Malachi read it twice, the way he'd read a clause he was about to be held to. A double negative. Whoever wrote it wanted a tired person to get the grammar wrong.

Arenya stood in the open rear door with the cold coming off the steel and leaned toward the cab's grille, close enough to smell the dust on it.

"Negative, M-Ops. D2-04 is not seated. Nobody in this truck is signing. Refused. Out loud, on the record, by the people who are here." She didn't say her name. She didn't say anyone's. Her voice came out the way it did when she read a correction back to an editor.

"Refused," Malachi said, level, to the cab. Then to the car: "If anyone's still breathing in there, say it with me." He held his watch in the light. "Twelve-fifteen. Ava, log it."

"Aquí no hay puerta," Kino said, from the chain-link, in the Spanish his Nana used at a threshold. The Lady's mark on his forearm went warm.

Ava kept her hand off the dropper and her eyes on Caleb's pupils. *Letting him shake.* "Second refusal," she told the cab, flat as dictation. "Not signing. Not seated."

And Caleb dug his heels into the floor of the back bench, put both palms on his thighs the way he used to hold a sling on a long walk, and counted eleven and reset, eleven and reset, against a hum that was wrong by half a beat. That half beat was the only thing he could hold. "Refused," he said. His voice cracked on it and he let it. "Not by proxy."

At 12:20 the radio held its breath through four refusals, and clicked.

"Refusal entered. Witnesses recorded." The dispatcher read them back, unhurried. A clerk checking a sheet. *Lindqvist, A. Strand, C.*

Neither had been said near the lid. Neither had needed to be.

Caleb's tremor stopped all at once, like a hand letting go of a rope. The ink dried to a dull brown. The chair was only wood and bolts again.

**Vacancy continues. Next sitting Mon 06:00. Bring the one who asks.**

Far down the spur, a pair of headlights came on with no engine behind them, wet yellow smears that swayed like a lantern. Then they went out.

* * *

Arenya picked the keys off the bumper with a tissue, not her fingers, and shut the door until the latch caught. She kept her palm on the steel.

"Okay." She said it to the car. "The receipt says *Alternate*, and the alternate is mine. I read my name aloud on Sunday and didn't say what it meant. I'm saying it now, because the Mill just put two of you in its book with a time beside you, and I'm not going to let it do that to anyone else by keeping quiet."

She wrote it in her notebook under 00:07. *12:22 — Alternate: A. Sowri. Named on receipt. Not signed.*

"You didn't have to say it," Malachi said. Quiet, not arguing in front of the truck. "The Mill already had your name. You made it a sworn statement with a time on it." A beat. "I'd have made the same choice. I'd still rather you hadn't."

12:25. Phones buzzed, his and Caleb's. Typhus, for once, sent the whole log without being asked, a wall of tags scrolling faster than anyone could read. Only the oldest line held still, in the Mill's own font.

**M-OPS · HOLDER: ADONIS · APPOINTMENT TORN 08/14 · REASON: "WHERE FROM?"**

Under it, in lowercase, the way Typhus typed when he was alone: *he found it and tore it up. who killed him. who is asking for his chair.*

Nobody answered him. The spur answered first.

* * *

Engines. Three of them, high beams already on.

Two pickups came down from the Pier 70 gate nose to tail, and a third killed its lights and idled across the tracks behind them. Chain-link on one side, the bay on the other, and eight rusted ties between the cars and the water. Five men got out of the lead truck with the unhurried walk of crews who'd been told the job was easy. Crown-leashed. The collars ticked under their jackets like cooling engines. A big man in a Dorsey Security windbreaker took his time.

"Evening. That truck's property of a client. Hand over the keys and the box, and nobody explains this to a cop." His eyes went to Arenya's coat pocket. He knew exactly which one.

Under her palm, the truck's rear door *thumped*, once, from the inside.

A second doorway had opened in the side of the truck: a square of lit nothing, the width of a man, with the Missing Man's cadence behind it. Nothing stood in it. It pulled at L-3 the way a drain pulls at water.

Arenya slapped her free hand to the steel beside it and pushed. "Ward of a Thousand Lights," she said, low. White came up from the rail ties, from the gravel, from nowhere, and Dorsey's high beams turned into candles. The square of nothing went hard-edged. It had a *seam*, down one side, like a badly hung door.

Malachi stepped out of the truck's shadow into the beams, hands open at his hips, his credentials in his left. He said what he had to say, flat, a federal hold, a closed spur. Dorsey read the date on the seal in the time it took to read a date.

"Expired Friday. Your ID died with the rest of your office, Agent." Almost kindly. A compact pistol came up, low, aimed at no one yet.

Then it was fast. Kino, with the jaguar behind his eyes, saw that two of the men weren't watching the car at all. They were watching the water. In the dark truck across the tracks a man was counting, not moving. "Count him too," Kino said, and the first Crown man lunged at the chain-link and rang his fist off a post.

Malachi asked Dorsey who'd told him the ID was dead, and watched his eyes instead of the gun. They flicked toward the dark truck, and back. *Fetch it, don't open it.* The man was the leash's last knot, and knew what happened to knots. The real hand on the box wasn't here. It was in the truck across the rail, counting for the doorway to finish pulling.

Ava, at Caleb's window, held up three fingers an inch from the glass. "Tell me your name. The one your mother used. Not the word it gave you." The man on the other side held. His lips shaped *Sit*. But when she barked at the one on the rear latch that his collar was on a timer, he jerked his hand back like the steel was hot.

Arenya found the seam with her eyes and put the Lance down it.

"That's a door with a bad hinge," she said. "And I'm the one who hangs it."

It went in narrow and hot, like a stripped wire leaving her hand. The white inside the rectangle skipped a frame. One corner peeled back like a damp label, wet dark behind it. The pull on L-3 cracked. A bolt in the chair's mounting ticked instead of shrieked.

In the dark truck, a stopwatch hand closed. Whoever was counting had what he wanted: how long the ward held when it was pushed. The engine caught.

Kino was already across the first tie, the jaguar's weight at his shoulder, planted in the road in front of the grille. "Look at me. Not the box." The truck lurched, and he went over the rail with the bumper a hand's breadth from his hip.

Dorsey fired twice. The first round took Malachi low on the left side.

It felt like a hot nail hammered through the coat. His knee went into a rail tie. The credentials spun out of his hand and landed face up in the ward-light, ordinary and dead.

Caleb came out of the car with the door crushing a Crown man's fingers, and got an arm under Malachi, and dragged. His grip slipped. The tremor cost him a step. Malachi ended half behind the wheel with his boots in the headlights, and the fourth truck hit the Mill truck broadside at walking speed, and L-3 slid its last foot and hung with a corner over the lit seam.

Malachi got the Glock out with his right hand. He sighted on a forearm, not a chest. The forearm of the man now climbing out of the dark cab with a hook pole and a stopwatch on a lanyard.

His left hand wouldn't close. The shot went wide into the seam, and the doorway swallowed it, and for half a second the knocking stopped. Then it came back faster, as though it had been fed.

The slide locked on a stovepipe jam.

Ava was on her knees in the gravel with her coat folded into a pad, both hands on the wound, her weight behind them. "Pressure. Don't stand up. Look at me, not the gun. Tell me the date."

"Twenty-ninth," said Malachi, through his teeth.

It was the thirty-first of nothing. It was the second of November. Ava let him have it.

* * *

12:31. The radio, once more.

"M-Ops line. Seat unclaimed. Box leaving. Final call: any alternate, or the box goes."

The seam brightened. The hum dropped, and everyone at the truck felt the cold *want* of it. The stopwatch man had the hook over L-3's handle and *pulled*, not toward the truck, toward the seam.

*Love,* said Abernathy, very low. *The cuff's gone still. That's what a thing does when it's listening.*

Arenya kept her name where it belonged, in her mouth.

"Refused," she said to the grille. "Not the alternate. Not signing. Not seated. The box stays, and nobody takes the chair. Put it in your book, twelve thirty-one, and don't come back for it."

Then she turned her palm on the steel and sent the Lance down the pole instead of the box. It went through the man's grip like a cut cable. The shaft went white at the join and fell away from the handle.

The lockbox stopped one inch short of the seam, held by a single bolt and the chair.

Ten feet from her hand, the stopwatch man looked up.
