The fog comes over the roofs of the Mission the way it always does on a Sunday, without knocking. It fills the gap between the buildings on the far side of Valencia and turns the streetlights into soft, useless coins.

My studio is too small for six people. Nobody says so.

The receipt sits on the kitchen table, face up, with Nana's pestle on one corner to keep it from curling. Beside it is my notebook, open to the page where I wrote *Choose who is there* and underlined it twice, as if the underlining might help.

It's 6:48. The creek asked for an answer by dusk. Dusk is now.

"Read it," Malachi says from the window. "Out loud. The date and the box. Don't paraphrase."

I don't paraphrase. I read it the way you'd read a parking ticket to a judge.

"*Oct 29. D2-04.* Then a line of dots. *c/o Lucero, I.* And then, *Alternate signatory: L. Sowri.* There's a tick next to it." I stop, and I let the tick sit there on the paper where everyone can see it. "I didn't sign that. I've never seen that form in my life. So either somebody forged my initial, or somebody's been planning for me to be the second pen on a box I'd never open."

The cuff on Abernathy's wrist glows through his sleeve. He doesn't look at it. He looks at the receipt, and then at the tick, and then he stops looking at anything, which is worse.

"Sowri's the light's name, love," he says, in the voice he uses when he's trying not to sound like anything at all. "The house it came from. So when that line says L. Sowri, somebody picked the family I come from and put a tick beside it."

"You know who L is?"

"I know I'm not telling you on a guess." The edges of him pull in, like a man stepping back from a bus. "Read the rest of it to Caleb. He's the one with the name in his mouth."

---

Caleb is on the arm of the couch with his hands jammed between his knees. He has the grey look he's had since the beach, like someone has rubbed out his edges and not finished. Ava is kneeling in front of him with two fingers on his wrist and her eyes on the second hand of her watch.

"Caleb," I say. "Say it back. Not to the paper. To me."

He looks up. It takes him a second to find my face. "Alternate signatory. L. Sowri."

"Does it sound like a favour?"

He thinks about it. I can see him not wanting to think about it. "It sounds like somebody already booked your pen," he says. His voice is steady enough for Ava's count, and she writes something down without looking up.

"Nobody signs," I say. "Ava, log that. Malachi, Abernathy, the name on that line is mine and I'm not handing it over tonight."

Ava's phone gives its soft chime. "Nineteen oh-two. Four hours thirty-eight." She says the time out loud so it's on the record. Then, quieter, "Typhus is still on step four."

As if he heard, Caleb's phone buzzes against his knee. He reads it with his thumb pressed to the glass. Then he reads it again.

"Lantern desk, twenty-two thirty, clean terminal, Building Two kiosk," he says. "Not before. And the eighteen-twelve request had no badge number. Just a tag." He swallows. "T4."

Malachi goes still in a way I've learned to notice. "Team Four was dissolved on Friday."

"Somebody's still answering to it," Caleb says, and he doesn't need to finish.

---

Kino has Ollie up off the couch before anyone sees him move. The hoodie hangs past Ollie's fingers. Ollie's eyes are open but not quite on anything, and he keeps shaping a word with his mouth, the way you'd chew something you're not sure is food.

"Nana's," Kino says. "Now. The good blanket and a kettle that doesn't whistle. You won't have to talk."

To me, low: "The back way. Up the Excelsior side. Nothing with a windshield."

"Go."

He doesn't say goodbye. He stops at the door, looks at Caleb once, and takes the stake out of his pocket long enough to show it to Ava. Then they're gone, down the back stairs, and the flat is about a third quieter and twice as heavy.

Ava writes it down. "Eighteen fifty-eight. Ollie out the back with Kino. Stake in pocket."

---

The thin light is on the sidewalk outside.

I don't go to the window. Abernathy does it for me, in a flicker, and then he's back at my shoulder with his voice pitched down.

"Doorway's on the kerb, love. Two blocks down, by the Lucero house. Upright, door-high, no wall behind it. It's taken a step. Along the seam, one at a time, like it's walking a line somebody drew for it."

"Toward us?"

"Toward the building. Not just near it." A pause. "It's paced itself to Kino's feet on the stairs. I didn't like that."

Malachi doesn't turn around. "Confirm it's moving toward this building, Abernathy. I want that on the record too."

"Confirmed. It's stopped at the gutter across from the gate. It's waiting for someone to open a door that isn't mine."

I hear the gate latch lift, somewhere below us. It lifts the way a hand does when it has been taught to be polite.

Ava's voice comes from the hall, very level. "Oven light's out. Hall lamp's out. Eleven seconds, I counted."

Then the knock.

Three knocks, spaced. Tap, pause, tap-tap. It comes through the front door like someone who knows exactly how much weight a door can take without sounding like a fist.

Ava, from the bedroom gap: "I'm logging the cadence. Tap, pause, tap-tap. The seam's at the lock. It hasn't come through."

I walk to the wall beside the hinge, where I can't be the first thing a gap in the wood can reach, and I put my back to the plaster.

"Malachi, don't touch the lock. Caleb, stay where Ava can see your hands." I keep my voice flat and loud enough for the seam to hear. "I'm not opening this. Not to a knock. You want a door held, you say whose door, and you say the name of the person you're here for. Not a tag. Not a team. A name."

Then, quieter, to the wood: "Lucero, I. Is that who's knocking?"

I don't wait for an answer. I wait for the next tap, and I count it against Ava's chime.

For eleven seconds, nothing.

Then a fourth knock, not the cadence. One soft tap, low, at knee height, as though whatever stands out there has bent down to say it.

Something slides under the door. It's a strip of thermal receipt, still warm, and it stops against the toe of my shoe.

I pick it up by the edges. The print is grey, the way a cartridge goes when it's nearly empty.

*c/o Lucero, I.*

A line of dots.

*Door: Pier 70, Bldg 12, side. 23:40.*

And under that, in a different ink, as if someone had added it by hand after the machine finished: *He will not ask twice. Neither will the chair.*

No signature line. No tick.

Outside, the thin light steps back across the gutter, one seam at a time, and stands where it stood. The oven light comes on. The hall lamp comes on. Ava's voice says, very quietly, "Nineteen eleven," and then nothing else for a while.

On the notebook, beneath *Choose who is there*, the ink is still wet. I did not write that. I know I didn't write it.

I fold the receipt in half, then in half again, and put it under the pestle with the other one.

Nobody signed. Nobody held a door.

But there's a place now, and an hour. And I haven't said no to either one out loud yet, which is the thing I've been trying not to think about since the beach.
