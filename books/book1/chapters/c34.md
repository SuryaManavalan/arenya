# Chapter Thirty-Four: Prestado

"Eleven again," Arenya said to the kitchen. "Nobody say it's a coincidence. I'll pay you a dollar."

Nobody took the dollar.

It was 7:40 on Monday morning, All Souls, and the Heron Thing was on her phone for the third time. Two million views. A jogger's shaky footage of the Candlestick mudflat, and a bird too tall by half, standing in four inches of water with its neck folded like a coat on a door. It turned its whole body toward Hunters Point. The water around its legs went glass-flat.

Eleven seconds. Then it was a heron again, and it lifted away, embarrassed, as if it had been caught.

"Six fifty-two," Kino told Ava, his left sleeve pulled down over his forearm. "Warm, like a cup somebody just set on it. It didn't fade when the bird lifted. It faded when the water moved."

Ava wrote *not to bird* and underlined *not* twice.

*Same stroke,* Abernathy said from the counter by the toaster, in the voice only Arenya could hear. *The waterline one. I know it, love. Can't tell you from where, and I'd thank the universe to stop making me try.*

She reached for the toast.

* * *

The morning came in pieces.

Appa came in from the garage holding his phone away from his face as if it were hot. A man he'd trained at PG&E in '09 had texted: *Meters on Hudson Ave all ticked back 11 sec at 6:52. also your block. sorry to bother you sir.* Appa wrote *6:52 HUDSON + HOME* on the envelope on the fridge, in his engineer's capitals, and pinned it back under the magnet.

Malachi's phone held three missed calls from a 703 number and a memo from someone named Corinne. "Tuesday at one I meet my handler," he said, and turned the phone face-down. "If I stop answering, assume I chose it."

At 8:50 her work email chimed. Elias Khatri. *Mr. Quinn asked me to find you. He'd like the coffee. Crissy Field, the warming hut, Wednesday 6:40 a.m.* A minute later, from a Gmail with no name: *I'd bring someone. — E.*

In his glass office, smelling of toner and somebody's banana, Teddy didn't fire her. "I'm doing worse. You're on the waterfront. Bayview, Hunters Point, the Heron Thing, whatever's making the Bay act like it drank something. Six hundred words, Tuesday at five."

"Thank you for doing worse," she said.

He slid a new keycard across the blotter, white, no photo, and told the window that her old one had pinged the Monolith lobby reader on Saturday. It had been in his drawer the whole time.

On the Montgomery platform she texted Malachi first, then Elias. *Yes. I'll bring someone.*

*6:40,* Elias wrote back. *He'll be early. He's always early when he's scared.*

At 11:58 Nana's answering machine took a call. Rodel, from a car with the window down, Colma traffic behind him. The pandit had come by the shop that morning with a man in a grey suit. The man had paid, and the pandit had given him a page out of the book. *"Then he bought a bag of oranges from the stand outside, like nothing happened. Tell your guest."*

Malachi put his cup back in the exact ring it had left on the table.

"Hale wears grey suits," Caleb said from the doorway. "I don't know it's him. I know he'd pay a man and then buy fruit."

Appa wrapped the cassette in a clean dish towel and put it in the freezer behind the peas, the way his mother had kept rupee notes. Under *6:52 HUDSON + HOME* he printed *ORANGES*.

* * *

Nana had added Susmithaa's name to the parish list in her own handwriting, at the bottom. At the four o'clock Mass the priest read it carefully and correctly and went on. Amma's hand closed on Appa's sleeve and stayed there.

Holy Cross at five-forty was a city lying down. White stone ran up the hill into the fog, every third grave had marigolds, and somebody's speaker played Vicente Fernández low. Arenya hated how well it fit.

Nana knelt at the Lucero plot and pulled grass from the edge of Ernesto's marker. *Esposo y Padre.* "Go say hello to your friends," she said, without turning. "Take Joaquín."

The friend was forty yards down the row. A newer black stone, no flowers. ADONIS QUINN. A grey pebble held down a square of printer paper.

Arenya pulled her cuff over her fingers and slid it out with her body between it and the hill. A sensor trace, LANTERN · BENCH 3, Halloween night. A slow line like a heartbeat with all the hurry taken out. Three peaks bracketed in blue ballpoint: *11.0 s.* The third bracket was pressed so hard the pen had nearly gone through.

On the back, in a loose hand written on a knee: *It's eleven seconds. It was always eleven, wasn't it. You wrote WHERE FROM and I thought you meant the money. I'm sorry I didn't come in May. — T.*

*Typhus,* she thought, and didn't say it.

A groundskeeper on a cart said a man in a good coat had sat on this wet grass for an hour that morning. No flowers. A pebble from the parking lot. "Some people don't know what to bring. I don't tell them."

She folded the page on its creases, put it back under the pebble, and told the granite she was sorry.

* * *

Amma didn't look at the canister. She looked at Arenya the way she looked at a pot she wasn't sure about.

"On the tenth day the ashes go to the water," she said. "My mother took my father to the sea at Visakhapatnam and came home and slept for the first time in eleven days. We are past the tenth day. I did not take her." Her thumb moved once on the white cloth. "Your father says the water is wrong. He doesn't say how."

Appa kept his left hand in his pocket, where the stain was.

"So I ask you, because you know these things now. Don't tell me you don't, kanna. I have watched you put salt on windowsills." Her voice didn't break. It narrowed. "If I give her to the water, is it the water that takes her? Or the other thing?" She turned her chin toward the Lucero stone. "Joaquín's grandmother says her husband has room at his feet. Not forever. *Prestado.*" She said it like a coin she wasn't sure was real. "You decide. I have tried since Sunday, and I can't."

Arenya had been counting the fog all afternoon. *Nine. Ten. Eleven.*

It didn't come. Twelve. Every candle on the hill stood straight up. Thirteen. Then the breath came all at once and bent every flame toward the Bay, and the white cloth on the canister lifted at one corner and pointed the same way.

Amma held it down with her hand.

"I don't know, Amma," Arenya said. "That's the true answer. But I saw that cloth. If something's waiting in the water, it's waiting for her by name, and I won't hand it a name it's asking for." She crouched at the edge of the grass. "Borrowed. Here, at his feet, until the water's clean. I'm the one who finds out when that is. When it's clean, I'll drive you to the sea myself. Visakhapatnam, if it has to be. And you'll sleep after."

Her voice wanted to go small. She put an edge on it.

"She won't mind the stranger. She'd have liked a man who was good with guests." She dipped her head to Nana. "Thank you for the room. We'll come back for her. I'm saying it out loud so it counts."

* * *

Nana cut a hand-sized hollow with a trowel and set the turf beside it like a lid. Amma knelt, sari in the wet grass, and set the canister down. Nobody handed it to anybody. They laid the turf back and covered it with marigolds.

The breath stopped.

Every candle stood dead straight. Then the fog came over the western ridge, not rolling but pouring, and where it touched section L the grass went black and seawater beaded in the carved letters of every name on every stone.

At the bottom of the row, something stepped out on legs too long for the hill. A heron, seven feet at the shoulder, grey as a parking-garage ceiling. Each step left a tideline across the graves. Its eye was a flat white coin. Down the slope a little boy said *big bird*, and his mother went very quiet.

The promise went off in Arenya's chest like a struck match.

Every candle flared white. Under the turf the canister glowed through the soil like a lamp under a blanket. And the hill, the whole old dark land from the ridge to the Bay, took one slow breath on *her* count.

Light by blood. Dark by birthplace. Both answered at once.

She stepped out of herself.
