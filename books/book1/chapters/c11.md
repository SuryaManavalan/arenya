# Chapter Eleven: Just Existing

The wall was starting to look unhinged.

What had once been a neat grid of names, dates, and Post-it flags had evolved into something more chaotic—organic. Threads of red yarn looped from thumbtack to thumbtack. Newspaper clippings. Screenshots. Blurry stills. Printouts from TikTok videos with freeze frames circled in highlighter. A new photo of Adonis Quinn had just joined the lineup.

Arenya stood back, squinting at it like it might blink and reveal something.

She’d added him to the center column—victims frozen in public. Intentional gaze. High visibility. She tried to trace a connection to Yuri, to the others. Was there a pattern to who died? Or just to how?

She moved names around, redrew arrows. The logic kept slipping just out of reach. Like something was missing. One piece. One truth that would make the rest lock into place.

She checked her phone again.

Still nothing from Elias.

The email had been sent two days ago. She kept telling herself that corporate gatekeeping moved slowly. That important people were busy. That no news wasn’t rejection. But every hour that passed gnawed a little deeper into her focus.

When her phone buzzed, her heart kicked.

New Notification: Saiesh

saiesh: wyd

She stared at it for a long second. No punctuation. No context. Just his usual semi-stoned check-in.

She didn’t respond.

Not because she was angry. Not because she didn’t care.

Because she couldn’t do that energy right now.

She didn’t want a couch hang or a half-baked snack idea or philosophical stoner tech babble.

She had momentum. Drive. Light.

And he—he dimmed it, just a little. Even when he didn’t mean to.

Arenya tossed her phone on the couch, pulled on her sneakers, and decided—without thinking too hard about it—that she needed to run.

She hadn’t gone running in weeks. Maybe months. But suddenly, the thought of movement, of letting her body burn while her mind spun, felt right.

The streets were dusky and sharp, fog hovering in low, ghostly streaks as she jogged out of the Mission, cutting through alleys and quiet avenues with no clear route. Just motion.

Her breath came hard and fast. Her arms pumped. Her brain was racing even faster.

What if Adonis saw someone he knew? What if the others did too?

She ran past glowing windows. A laundromat. A café closing for the night. A woman dragging a trash can to the curb.

What if the pattern wasn’t in the victims—but in who they saw?

She ran harder.

For a moment, the thoughts dropped away, replaced by the sound of her shoes on pavement, her breath in her ears, her heart thudding like war drums. She felt like she was outrunning something. Or chasing it.

The mountain.

It wasn’t behind her anymore. It was inside her.

When she finally slowed to a stop, knees aching, lungs clawing for air, she realized she was near Dolores Park. She stood under the dim orange of a streetlight, sweat cooling on her arms, chest heaving.

She felt wired. Clean. Clear-eyed.

Still nothing from Elias.

But the silence didn’t matter quite as much now. Because for the first time in days, her mind wasn’t spiraling.

It was sharpening.

* * *

The bakery smelled like sugar and stress—like it always did after sunset.

Arenya stopped in, sweaty and wind-flushed from the run, hair half-tied and hoodie zipped to her chin. Ava barely blinked when she walked in. At this point, she was part of the décor.

“You again,” Ava said, handing her a lukewarm oat chai before she even asked. “Tell me this is a social visit and not another ‘do you remember how dead that guy looked’ chat.”

Arenya gave a sheepish shrug. “Can’t it be both?”

Ava rolled her eyes, but the corners of her mouth twitched like she was trying not to smile.

Over the past few weeks, their odd little rapport had formed—part shared trauma, part casual sarcasm. Arenya felt guilty that every conversation always seemed to drift back to Yuri and the incident. But Ava had never pushed her away. She seemed to weirdly get it.

Still, she felt the need to pivot today.

“You were saying something about a date last time,” Arenya said, stirring her drink. “How’d that go?”

Ava groaned. “Ugh. I swear, every guy in this city thinks they’re the second coming of the singularity. He spent twenty minutes explaining his ‘emotional alignment model’ for relationships. Emotional. Alignment. Model.”

“Jesus.”

“He said it had ‘use cases.’”

“Christ.”

“Then he asked if I’d ever had a polyamorous phase—unironically.”

Arenya snorted into her cup. “Men are so weird in this city.”

They sat near the front window. The warm interior lighting glowed against the gathering fog outside, the glass fogging in soft circles from the night air.

After a few minutes of chatter, Arenya leaned forward.

“Okay, one grim thing and then I’ll stop—I promise.”

Ava gave her a mock look of pain. “Hit me.”

“Did Yuri… know anyone here that day? Anyone he might’ve come in to meet? Regulars? Friends? Staff? Anything at all?”

Ava shook her head. “He was polite but private. No small talk, no hangouts. Came in like clockwork, ordered, sat alone. I honestly don’t think he had plans to meet anyone.”

“Okay. And—just making sure—you don’t know of any connection to Monolith?”

“Monolith like the space people?” Ava blinked. “God, no. He was an architect. Retired. He once complained about how satellites made the night sky too busy.”

Arenya sighed, disappointed but not surprised.

She didn’t want to make the conversation just about death again, so she nudged it back. “Anyway. Your dating life’s more terrifying than my conspiracy wall.”

“That’s the nicest thing anyone’s said to me this week.”

Ava nodded out the window toward the street. “Speaking of terrifying men—what is that guy doing?”

Arenya followed her gaze.

Across the street, a man stood with his hands in his pockets, motionless. Sunglasses. High-collared zip-up. Generic tech startup posture. The kind of man you see ten times a day in San Francisco without registering—until he doesn’t move. He was just there. Like a screensaver left running too long.

“How long’s he been standing there?” Arenya asked.

“Since I went on break. So, at least twenty minutes. Maybe longer. Hasn’t checked his phone once.”

“Creepy.”

“Just… existing.”

Arenya watched him a little longer. Something about him felt off—not overtly sinister. Just too blank. As if he’d been built to blend in, but whoever programmed him forgot to include idle animations.

He turned. Walked away. Calm. Steady. Like it was nothing.

Arenya’s stomach turned, just a little.

He looked familiar.

Not personally. Not in the way you know a face.

In the way you know a type.

Like she’d passed ten versions of him during her run.

Vague. Inoffensive.

Forgettable by design.

Her fingers curled tighter around the warm cup. She shook it off.

Probably nothing.
