# Chapter Eight: Expression

It had been twenty-three days since the footage.

Three weeks of absolutely nothing.

No new victims. No new footage. No new anything.

Arenya kept checking her inbox like it was a crime scene she might catch in progress. She reread her notes so many times she started to resent her own handwriting. She walked past the bakery twice a week. Not to do anything—just to look. To see if something had shifted. It never had.

She was tired. Jittery. Half-convinced she’d imagined everything.

The wall of notes in her apartment started to feel more like a shrine to a delusion.

And then, this morning, it happened.

A death. In the city.

In public.

Caught on video.

Same signature.

Same stillness.

Arenya found out via a text from Saiesh, who simply sent a TikTok link and the words: you’re going to want to see this.

She watched it on mute, eyes wide.

The clip had already racked up over six million views. It showed a crowded sidewalk in front of some swanky bar in Lower Nob Hill—some launch party for a startup or a fashion collab, it wasn’t clear. What was clear was the man standing near the curb, frozen like a statue.

Young. Blond. Ridiculously symmetrical face. White button-down open at the collar. He wasn’t just still—he was locked. And his face—

It wasn’t fear. Or confusion.

It was anger.

Eyes hard. Jaw clenched. Like he was glaring at someone right before the punch.

Then he dropped.

The camera caught the scream of someone nearby. Caught the shout for help. But it was too late. He was already gone.

The caption read:

Wtf is happening in SF??? Bro just froze and DIED mid-argument???

#OD #Possessed #TyphusCousin #Monolith #RichPeopleThings

Arenya exhaled, heart thudding.

He hadn’t been arguing. He hadn’t moved at all.

But he’d been focused—locked onto someone in the crowd.

Someone the camera didn’t catch.

Someone who, if the pattern held, was never seen again.

The dead man’s name was Adonis Quinn.

He was twenty-seven. A mechanical systems engineer for Monolith.

And more importantly—he was the cousin of Typhus Quinn, the face and founder of the $80 billion space tech company.

This wasn’t going to stay quiet.

Already, the major media outlets were calling it a drug overdose. Adonis was rich, young, worked in tech—easy story. Something about “a history of reckless behavior,” a leaked video from a party three years ago, a blurry photo of a pill bottle in the background of an Instagram story.

Neat. Marketable.

Arenya didn’t buy a word of it.

The face he made before collapsing—there was no panic. No high. No haze.

It was rage.

Intentional. Directed.

She rewound the video and played it again, watching him scan the crowd like a predator. Like he knew someone was there.

* * *

This was it.

She felt it immediately—buzzing beneath her skin, behind her ribs.

This was her opening.

All of the major outlets were spinning the same sanitized headline: Tragic Overdose Claims Young Engineer. Most leaned into the Monolith angle, all with the same undertone—privilege, recklessness, another golden boy gone too soon. They didn’t question it. They didn’t ask about the stillness. The expression.

They didn’t know what she knew.

They hadn’t seen Yuri. Or the yoga instructor. Or the woman in the park who stared at the sky like it had spoken her name.

But she had. And this time, everyone was watching.

Arenya stood in the center of her apartment, adrenaline pulsing, half-dressed, phone still in hand. She paced once. Twice.

This could be it. The thread that unravels the whole thing. But only if I can get to the family.

She needed context. Confirmation. She needed to talk to someone who knew Adonis—who could tell her whether he’d had health issues, what he was doing that night, who he was meeting.

But this wasn’t a neighborhood witness or a grieving sibling who’d answer a knock on the door. This was elite. Old-money tech-royalty. The Quinns probably had two layers of PR between them and their own thoughts. And with Typhus Quinn being who he was, they would already be circling the wagons.

Arenya rubbed her temples.

She didn’t have the reach. Not on her own. Her stories were still being published under the “Lifestyle & Wellness” vertical. She couldn’t even get press clearance for a city hall briefing last month.

And then the thought hit her—cold and irritating and completely correct.

There’s only one person in this building with access to that world.

She closed her eyes, sighed hard, and said it aloud just to get it over with.

“Brooke.”

Brooke. Who had once done a panel with Typhus Quinn at a clean tech summit. Brooke, who had interviewed the Prime Minister of Sweden and still made it sound like a chore. Brooke, who wore beige like a weapon and always smelled vaguely expensive.

Arenya opened her laptop, stared at the Monolith homepage, then opened a new tab and typed Brooke Chatterjee | Contact into the internal company directory.

Her hand hovered over the keyboard. For a second, pride got in the way.

She could try to go around. Fake a connection. Lie. But she didn’t have time for the long way. Not now. Not when the trail was still warm.

She pulled up the company Slack. Typed the message.

arenya.s: Hey, random—but do you still have contacts at Monolith or in the Quinn orbit? Would love to pick your brain if you have a second.

She hovered over send.

Then clicked.

Three seconds later, the little “Brooke is typing…” bubbles appeared.

Arenya took a deep breath. Straightened up.

The mountain wasn’t going to climb itself.
