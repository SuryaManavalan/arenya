# Chapter Ten: Less Elon, More Heart

Arenya had three coffees, six open tabs, and a Google Doc titled Monolith / Quinn — Deep Context that was already 3,200 words long and mostly bullet points.

She was officially down the rabbit hole.

It started with the basics: Monolith began as a lean, clever operation launching micro-satellites using leftover payload capacity on NASA rockets. Typhus Quinn was twenty-five at the time. By twenty-eight, he’d moved Monolith from scrappy startup to private contractor with global clients and government bids. Now, Monolith was a tech-industrial empire.

Satellite automation. Self-building rovers. AI-managed off-world manufacturing systems. Most recently, they’d gone all in on asteroid mining—specifically targeting mineral-rich near-Earth objects. And somehow, they’d made it sexy. Sleek press kits. Hypnotic visuals. Big promises about green energy, clean futures, limitless potential.

Space. But make it sustainable.

It was polished, confident, and a little terrifying. They weren’t just a tech company anymore. They were infrastructure. Power. Orbiting influence.

And at the center of it all was Typhus.

Every article, podcast, interview said the same things: genius. Visionary. Ruthless. He did the work. Wrote the code. Built the engines. Spoke in blunt, elegant phrases that made people believe he knew what was coming before anyone else did.

He’d grown up middle class in Marin—his mother taught at a magnet school, his father was an electrical contractor. No legacy. No trust fund. Just intellect, drive, and a cousin who everyone agreed was practically his brother.

Adonis.

The grief, if it was real, would be buried under control. Press statements. Silence. But it had to be there.

Arenya stared at a photo of the two of them from some Monolith awards gala. Typhus in black. Adonis in grey. Both with the same sharp jawline and aloof, don’t-touch-me confidence. Except Adonis was smiling. Really smiling. And Typhus, standing slightly behind him, had his hand on his shoulder—not possessive. Protective.

They were close.

Which meant he would care.

Which meant he might listen.

If she could just find the right way in.

* * *

Meanwhile, work was on autopilot.

Teddy hadn’t bothered her in days. She’d written three articles last week using ChatGPT and two keyword spreadsheets and hadn’t even read them after hitting submit. One was about how hedgehogs reduce stress. Another ranked the top ten self-watering plant pots. Teddy loved them all.

“You’re on fire,” he said over Slack, followed by three rocket emojis. “Maybe a piece on ‘How to Build Trust with Your Cat’ next?”

“Sure,” she’d replied. “Working title: Don’t Blink First.”

Teddy responded: “Genius.”

It was almost too easy.

The job no longer felt like a real thing. It was just background noise. Something to keep the lights on while she went hunting in deeper waters.

* * *

She scrolled back through the Monolith homepage again, her eyes landing on a small banner ad for an upcoming symposium: Beyond Earth: Private Exploration and the New Mineral Age.

Typhus Quinn was listed as keynote.

It was in four days.

The event wasn’t public—but she had a contact now. Elias Khatri. She still hadn’t heard from him. But maybe this would push things forward. Maybe it was time to follow up.

She opened a new message. Kept it short. Direct.

Hi Elias,

Just saw the speaker list for Beyond Earth this weekend. Any chance I could attend—unofficially, or under NDA? Just to listen.

Still hoping for a chance to speak with Mr. Quinn when the time is right.

Best,

Arenya

She hovered over send.

Then clicked.

* * *

The cursor still blinked on her screen when she heard the click of heels approaching.

Brooke.

Arenya braced instinctively, half-expecting some passive-aggressive jab about deadlines or article formatting. But Brooke stopped beside her desk and leaned one manicured hand on the edge like she had nothing better to do.

“So,” she said, voice smooth as almond milk, “how did your little field trip to Monolith go?”

Arenya blinked, caught off guard. “You heard back from Elias?”

Brooke arched a brow. “I arranged the meeting. I like to follow through.”

“Oh. Right. It… went okay.”

Brooke gave her a look like don’t give me journalist vagueness.

“I met with the head of security,” Arenya said. “He didn’t throw me out, so I’m calling that a win. He gave me one of those heavy business cards that feels like a warning.”

Brooke smiled faintly, clearly pleased.

“And what exactly are you chasing?” she asked, brushing invisible lint from her sleeve. “You mentioned odd deaths last time. You still on that?”

Arenya hesitated, then nodded. “Yeah. I think Adonis Quinn’s death fits the pattern. There’s something bigger happening. Everyone’s treating it like a tragic overdose but… it’s not. Not if you’ve been paying attention.”

Brooke studied her for a second. That unreadable, calculating gaze she had. The one that could scan a press release and know exactly what wasn’t being said.

To Arenya’s surprise, she didn’t scoff. She didn’t mock.

Instead, she said, “Interesting.”

And she meant it.

“Typhus is interesting, too,” she added after a beat. “I’ve only spoken to him once. Years ago. Briefly. Some panel thing.”

“What was he like?” Arenya asked.

Brooke tilted her head slightly, as if retrieving the file from her mental archive. “Focused. Definitely intelligent. A little awkward, honestly. Not in a bad way. Just… you know. Tech-brain. Talks like his mouth is catching up to something his neurons finished an hour ago.”

Arenya smirked. “So, nerdy.”

“Deeply. But serious. Intent. There’s something about him that feels… off-the-grid, even when he’s onstage. Doesn’t small-talk well. Doesn’t play the room.”

That tracked.

Brooke tapped a nail against her own chin. “Think Elon Musk, but less plans for world domination and more heart. Haha.”

Arenya visibly flinched.

Brooke laughed. “Yeah, I know. Gross comparison. But you get what I mean.”

She didn’t. Not fully. But she hoped Typhus was less Elon and more… human. More heart.

As Brooke turned to leave, Arenya surprised herself.

“Hey, Brooke.”

She paused, glanced back over her shoulder.

“Thanks,” Arenya said. And this time, it wasn’t sarcastic. It wasn’t performative. She meant it.

Brooke smirked. “Don’t mention it.”

And then she was gone—heels clicking into the distance, sleek and untouchable as always.
