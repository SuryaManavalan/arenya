# Chapter Twelve

She was halfway to Saiesh’s place when the ping hit her phone.

New Email: Elias Khatri

Subject: Quinn. Tonight.

Her heart dropped into her stomach.

Typhus is attending the keynote. If you can be on campus within the hour, I can grant you a guest badge. Discreet. No recording devices. Just a conversation. If he’s open to it.

This is your window.

Don’t miss it.

Arenya didn’t think. Didn’t text Saiesh.

Didn’t even finish crossing the street.

She pivoted hard, turned around, and started walking fast in the opposite direction, already dialing up a Waymo. Her fingers trembled.

She ran the rest of the way home.

Once inside, she tore open her closet and glared at the absolute lack of options. Nothing screamed credible enough for a billionaire, but she managed to piece together something clean and sharp: fitted black pants, an open-collared blouse she only wore to weddings or job interviews, a tailored blazer. She hesitated… then added the boots with just a bit of a heel.

When she looked in the mirror, she winced.

Professional. A little enticing.

Ugh.

Brooke would be proud. Or horrified.

She threw on some light makeup with the kind of speed only crisis could inspire. Tightened her ponytail. Spritzed perfume. Regretted the perfume.

As she stepped into the car, she caught her reflection in the window.

She looked exactly like the kind of woman who was about to network with a billionaire she claimed to hate.

“God, I hate this,” she muttered.

But she needed this. Not for a byline. Not for status.

For Yuri. For Adonis. For the people no one else thought twice about.

For whatever this thing was that had been whispering beneath the surface of the city for weeks now.

If it took a little self-compromise to crack something open, then so be it.

The car sped toward the Monolith campus as the sky turned to fire behind the skyline.

She gripped her bag tighter.

This wasn’t a game anymore.

She was getting in.

* * *

All she could think on the ride over was: Be open. Be inviting. Don’t freeze. Don’t talk too much. Don’t talk too little. Be confident. World-class confident.

She mumbled it like a prayer. Like armor.

You belong here. You deserve this.

The Monolith campus glowed under sunset light, all matte glass and clean white angles like it had been grown in a lab rather than built. Everything looked frictionless. Unreasonably pristine. Even the grass outside looked like it had signed an NDA.

She was met at the front entrance by a young man in a perfectly fitted graphite-grey jacket, no name tag, no expression.

“Ms. Sowri?” he said. She nodded. “Right this way.”

He moved fast. Not rushed, just efficient. As if this entire encounter had already happened on a spreadsheet somewhere.

The halls were quiet, the lights soft and even. The walls had embedded screens cycling through sleek animations of satellites, asteroid mining rigs, digital terrain scans. Arenya felt like she’d stepped into the future—and it hated her for being wrinkled.

Her thoughts were bouncing like loose screws in a box: What if he doesn’t take me seriously? What if I sound like a conspiracy nut? What if I miss it—this one chance?

They passed through two layers of security. Then a corridor with smoked glass walls. Then another door.

Elias was waiting inside.

He looked tired. As usual. But he offered her a nod that almost counted as warm.

“You made it,” he said.

“Barely.”

“This is going to be fast,” Elias continued, stepping beside her as her escort peeled away. “Typhus agreed to five minutes. Before the panel. That’s all I could get. Make it count.”

Five minutes.

Arenya’s heart pounded against her ribcage. She nodded.

“Understood.”

Elias scanned her one last time—not suspiciously, but as if double-checking that she wasn’t going to combust under pressure. Then he turned and led her toward the backstage corridor.

Each step felt like a countdown.

* * *

Typhus Quinn approached from the far end of the corridor, flanked by two assistants in slim suits with tablets in hand. The moment she saw him, Arenya realized—immediately—that no one’s descriptions had done him justice.

Gone was the image she’d cobbled together: the awkward, brilliant, hoodie-clad engineer. What walked toward her was clean-cut and composed, taller than she expected—6’2”, maybe taller. His slick black hair curled slightly at the ends, not overly styled, just enough to seem effortless. His suit was dark and sharply tailored, minimalist in a way that whispered obscene wealth.

And his face…

Sharp. Calm. Guarded.

He didn’t look like a genius. He looked like a man who had long since stopped trying to prove he was one.

“Ms. Sowri,” he said, his voice low and even as Elias introduced them.

“Mr. Quinn,” she replied, trying not to choke on the absurdity of her own presence.

He motioned toward a quiet alcove, off to the side. The two assistants disappeared like stagehands. Elias nodded once and stayed just outside earshot.

They stood in the hush together. Awkward. Civil.

She cleared her throat.

“First, thank you for this. I know you’re busy and I only have—”

“Five minutes,” he said. “So far, you’ve used twelve seconds.”

Right. Okay. She needed to not melt.

“I’m not here to write what everyone else is writing,” she said quickly. “I’m not chasing a drug narrative. I don’t think that’s what happened to Adonis. At all.”

He didn’t respond. Just looked at her.

And in that moment, the weight of it hit her like a wave.

Kill the rich. That was one of her old tweets. She remembered the post. It was from college, written during a protest, half-performance, half-rage. Now here she was. Wearing nice boots. Offering her warmth to Typhus Quinn like they were just two people trying to understand something.

She was supposed to hate people like him.

And maybe she still did.

But there was something about his eyes—tired, deeply focused, and behind it all, hurt.

He wasn’t showing it, not exactly. But she could feel it. The tension just below the surface. The effort it took for him to be fine right now, moments before stepping onstage to represent everything.

“I was close with my cousin,” he said, finally. “I don’t do many of these panels anymore, but this one mattered to him. So here I am.”

“I’m sorry,” she said, softer. “You must be carrying a lot right now.”

She hesitated, then—channeling Brooke—reached out and touched his arm gently, just above the elbow.

He flinched.

Just slightly. Like the contact surprised him. Like he wasn’t used to people doing that.

She started to pull back—but he didn’t move away.

His shoulders relaxed, just a fraction. And he nodded, once. A thank you he didn’t say aloud.

A beat passed between them.

She took a breath.

“I don’t think Adonis’s death was random,” she said carefully. “I’ve been researching a string of similar cases in the city. There’s a pattern. And his death fits it—not the overdose explanation, but… something else. Something more deliberate.”

His expression changed.

Not shock. Not curiosity.

Something more dangerous.

Scrutiny.

“You really think so?” he asked. Quiet. But there was a sudden sharpness behind it.

Arenya’s heart leapt into her throat. She felt her mouth dry. She was about to backpedal—say she could explain, or soften it—

But he was still watching her.

Thinking.

For a moment so long she forgot how to breathe.

And then—

His gaze softened again. His lips twitched into the barest ghost of a smile.

“If you really think that,” he said, “and you seem like someone who means what she says… we can talk more.”

She blinked. “I’d like that.”

“Over coffee, maybe,” he added, voice almost amused.

And then—chaos.

“Typhus, you’re on in thirty seconds!” a PA called out, rushing into view. “They’re mic’d and waiting!”

He turned, nodded once, and gave Arenya a glance that felt almost like an inside joke. Like they had an understanding now.

“Ms. Sowri,” he said in parting, with a tiny smile.

And then he was gone. Swept up by staff, walking briskly into the lights and cameras and everything she wasn’t a part of.

Arenya stood frozen in the quiet that followed.

Not smiling. Not moving.

Then—shit—she realized.

She still wasn’t smiling.

She scrambled to correct it, flashing a forced grin at no one in particular, cheeks burning.

Then she let it drop, slowly.

She had no idea what had just happened.

But she knew one thing:

She was in.

Elias was waiting by the hallway where she’d entered. His expression was unreadable—but only for a moment.

As she approached, he gave her a look of genuine surprise, eyebrows lifted just slightly.

“Well,” he said, “I’ll admit, I wasn’t sure how that would go.”

“Neither was I,” Arenya said, still stunned, still half-smiling from whatever the hell that had just been.

“He doesn’t usually extend things like that. Or soften.”

“He’s… different in person,” she said carefully. “Intense. But… human.”

Elias nodded. “He respected something in you.”

She didn’t know how to respond to that.

They walked back toward the exit in silence, his pace relaxed now, the tension from earlier loosened. The halls still glowed with their perfect white light. She passed the same digital screens, but now she felt like she had seen behind one of them. Like something private had cracked open and let her glimpse a more vulnerable orbit.

Just as they passed the wide doors to the auditorium, she heard the voice of the moderator echo out:

“Before we begin—Typhus, I want to start by addressing the tragedy. The entire world was shocked by the passing of your cousin, Adonis Quinn, and I think everyone here is wondering… was it an overdose, as some outlets have reported?”

Arenya stopped for a half second. Her chest went cold.

She didn’t turn back. Didn’t linger. But she kept walking, the weight of that question pressing in behind her like a tide.

She didn’t know Typhus Quinn. Not really.

But she’d seen it—his grief, not loud but anchored, like a stone he was carrying alone. And now it was just another story. Another headline. Another excuse for the world to feast.

She stepped into the outside air, the doors closing behind her with a soft hydraulic hiss.

Her heels clicked once against the stone path.

Then silence.

She didn’t smile this time.

She just breathed.
