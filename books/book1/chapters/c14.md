# Chapter Fourteen: The Dream Remembers

She was scared.

Not the sharp, immediate kind of fear—no sirens, no screams.

This was the cold, creeping kind. The kind that took root.

Malachi’s voice still echoed in her head: Please don’t make me come back.

Arenya stared at her wall.

The wall.

A monument to weeks of obsession. Of names and string and grainy images, of deaths that didn’t make sense and glances that had meant everything.

Now, it looked like a liability.

She stood still for a long time before moving. Then slowly, deliberately, she began to take it all down.

Post-its. Printouts. Tape.

One by one.

Peeling back the layers like removing bandages from a wound that hadn’t healed.

But before the last photo came down, she took out her phone.

Click.

A full shot of the madness.

Proof that she hadn’t imagined it.

Then it was gone.

The wall bare again, except for faint outlines where the light hadn’t touched.

She sat on her couch, breath shallow.

This was stupid. Dangerous.

She knew that.

She knew that the right thing to do—the safe thing—was to let go. To vanish back into her hamster listicles and hollow workplace praise. To delete the photo and pretend none of it had ever happened.

But then she thought of Yuri. Of Adonis. Of the bakery. Of that man standing outside Ava’s window for too long.

And then she thought of Typhus Quinn.

He had listened. Really listened.

He had said: If you really believe this… we can talk more.

That wasn’t nothing.

She closed her eyes and pressed her fingers to her temples.

Too much.

Too loud.

Too much right now.

So she did the thing she always did when her life started to fray at the edges.

She slept.

Not to escape—not exactly.

But because sleep was the only place she could be both elsewhere and herself.

* * *

The dream came, like it always did.

Amorphous at first—colors with no name, voices without mouths, places folding over themselves like paper lanterns.

Then, clarity.

She was climbing.

The mountain again.

Endless. Ancient. Breathing.

It loomed high above the clouds, jagged and impossible, but she was climbing. Fingers raw. Legs shaking. Wind tearing at her face like it wanted to erase her.

It wasn’t just a mountain.

It was alive.

But asleep.

And she…

She was its breath.

They breathed together. Her lungs pulling air as its invisible body swelled and rested beneath her.

Then—a tremor.

Deep. Subtle. A sigh in stone.

Another.

Louder.

Sharper.

It was stirring.

The world quaked beneath her. Her grip slipped. Her balance shattered.

She tumbled.

Fell.

Weightless.

Spinning.

Until—

The fork.

The same fork.

To the right: the waterslide. Glistening, easy, sun-warmed, gleaming with soft promises.

To the left: the mountain. Towering. Menacing. Waiting.

She didn’t move. Just stood at the crossroads again, chest heaving.

Then—

She woke.

Jolted upright in bed, sweat on her neck, breath ragged. The bedside lamp buzzed gently. Her sheets tangled around her like vines.

Outside, the city exhaled through the cracked window.

She sat there, staring at nothing.

She was afraid.

And she was awake.

And she wasn’t stopping.

* * *

Too much was going on.

Too many thoughts. Too many echoes. Too many possible futures, all flickering like bad signal in her brain.

So she didn’t choose one.

She slept.

Through Saturday. Through Sunday.

No more intense dreams. No more mountain. No more crossroads. Just soft, flickering nonsense—like her mind was buffering.

She barely got up except to drink water, stumble to the bathroom, microwave something bland, and crawl back into bed. No phone. No laptop. No wall of names watching her. Just the sheets and the silence and the hum of a world she wasn’t ready to reenter.

It wasn’t avoidance. Not exactly.

It was… delay.

A temporary hold. Like putting life on mute.

Because when she was asleep, there were no decisions to make.

No looming agents.

No haunted billionaires.

No cracked-open truths waiting for her to claim them.

There was only weightlessness.

And for one weekend—just one—that was enough.
