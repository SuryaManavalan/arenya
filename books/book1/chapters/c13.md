# Chapter Thirteen

She walked home.

The Waymo had been for the look. The suit, the perfume, the whole performance—crafted for that five-minute moment. But now that it was over, the act was heavy. Stupid. Her feet hurt. Her makeup felt like a mask. And she didn’t need another receipt to remind her that she was still, fundamentally, broke.

But god—the butterflies.

They’d been flapping since Typhus said coffee. Since he looked at her like she mattered. Like her theory mattered. It wasn’t just adrenaline anymore—it was ignition.

She cut through the quieter streets. Ducked the noise. Let herself daydream just a little.

By the time she reached her building and climbed the stairs, her body was tired, but her mind was on fire.

She reached her door.

It was slightly ajar.

She froze.

Arenya never left it open.

Her heart ticked once, hard.

Ugh. Saiesh.

She pushed the door open. “Saiesh, are you serious?” she called out, already half-annoyed, half-exhausted. “You can’t keep breaking into my—”

She stopped cold.

Saiesh was there. Sitting on her couch. Shoulders tense, legs stiff. Not lounging. Not stoned. Just still.

And not looking at her.

He was looking past her. At the wall.

No—not at the wall.

At the man standing in front of it.

A tall figure in a dark coat. His back to them, one hand raised as he gently adjusted a pinned note. He was studying the conspiracy wall with quiet fascination, like it was an art installation or a piece of abstract code only he could read.

When he spoke, his voice was calm. Even.

“Sit down, please.”

Arenya didn’t move.

The man turned, slowly.

He was tall. Lean. His features sharp but unassuming. He looked like someone who could vanish into a crowd—until he didn’t. His eyes were too clear. Too focused. There was something in them that said: I know things you haven’t even begun to fear yet.

“Who the hell are you?” she asked.

He took a slow step forward. Didn’t raise his voice.

“My name is Malachi Shawani. I’m with the Special Intelligence Division of the Department of Energy.”

She blinked. “That’s… not a thing.”

He almost smiled. “That’s what makes it special.”

He gestured again. “Please. Sit.”

Saiesh finally looked at her. The fear in his eyes wasn’t loud. But it was real. His usual haze was gone.

Arenya sat.

She didn’t know why. But she did.

Malachi stepped closer to the wall, fingers brushing over the image of Adonis Quinn. Then over Yuri. Then the one grainy still of the men in the café.

“This,” he said softly, “is very good work.”

Arenya swallowed. “What do you want?”

He turned to face her. Eyes calm. Voice clear.

“I want you to stop.”

Malachi walked slowly along the wall, not quite looking at her. His fingers drifted across the edge of one of the photos, pausing on Adonis’s frozen expression. He didn’t touch it—just let the moment hang in the space between them.

Arenya stared at him. “You want me to stop.”

He nodded once. “Yes.”

“Stop investigating people who’ve died?”

“Stop digging into things that weren’t meant to be unearthed.”

“That’s not an answer.”

“That’s the point,” he said smoothly, finally turning back toward her. “I’m not here to answer your questions, Ms. Sowri. I’m here to let you know that you’ve done enough.”

“And if I don’t?”

His gaze didn’t waver. “Then we will have to reconsider the arrangement.”

“What arrangement?”

He smiled, just a fraction. “The one where you get to keep going about your life, comfortably unaware of the larger forces at play.”

A chill climbed her spine.

“You’ve been following me,” she said quietly.

Malachi’s head tilted, amused. “If I were following you, you wouldn’t have noticed.”

Silence stretched.

He walked over to the couch and sat—not beside her, not close, but deliberately there. Still calm. Still composed. Like a man used to being listened to without needing to shout.

“I’m not here to threaten you,” he said. “Not really. You’re smart. And curious. That’s a dangerous combination. Especially now.”

Arenya narrowed her eyes. “What does that mean?”

“It means that the narrative being told to the public serves a purpose. The story of a drug overdose, a tragic fluke. People need closure. Clean ends.”

“You’re saying it’s not true.”

“I’m saying you already know that.”

She felt her jaw tighten.

“So what are you? Government clean-up crew? Some off-the-books agency that specializes in… gaslighting journalists?”

“I’m just here to make sure you don’t ask questions you won’t like the answers to.”

“Try me.”

Malachi leaned forward just slightly. The light hit his eyes in a way that made them seem glassy. Too focused. Too still.

“There are things in motion that are far older, far colder, and far less patient than the human mind is built to comprehend. You think you’ve found a thread. You think it’s yours to pull.”

He paused.

“It isn’t.”

The room was silent.

Arenya could feel Saiesh’s breath, shallow beside her. She didn’t know what to say next—not because she was afraid, but because some primal part of her was telling her to listen.

Malachi stood. Smoothed the front of his coat. He stepped back toward the door.

“I’m not your enemy,” he said. “But I’m also not your friend.”

And then, as casually as if he were commenting on the weather:

“Please don’t make me come back.”

He opened the door, stepped out, and was gone.

No badge left behind.

Just the echo of what he’d said. And what he hadn’t.

* * *

The silence lingered after the door clicked shut. Long enough that Arenya thought maybe Saiesh wouldn’t say anything.

He did.

“Dude. What the fuck.”

She turned slowly. He was still on the couch, hunched forward, hands clenched between his knees. His leg was bouncing—nervous energy twitching out of him.

“That guy was not normal,” he said. “He didn’t even show a badge. They’re supposed to show a badge. That’s, like, rule number one of fake government encounters.”

“Maybe he’s real.”

“No. No no no. That dude? In my expert opinion, he’s a goon. Like, capital G. Sent to mess with you, freak you out, and probably erase your hard drive if you hadn’t walked in.”

He was talking fast now. Rattling.

“This isn’t just some thing we were doing on the side anymore, okay? It’s not fun and sketchy and illegal-in-a-cool-way. Someone noticed. And not someone good.”

Arenya said nothing. She was still staring at the door.

Saiesh kept going.

“I mean, come on. You’re telling me the Department of Energy has a spooky backroom agent division? That’s comic book shit. That guy probably works for a shell corporation with a three-letter acronym and a blank website.”

“I know, Saiesh,” she snapped.

He froze. She didn’t usually snap.

“I know it’s not a game anymore,” she said, standing up, voice sharp now. “I’m the one who’s been putting this together. I’m the one who’s been digging. You think I don’t realize how serious this is?”

“You sure don’t act like it!” he fired back, louder than she expected. “Where were you today? We were supposed to hang out. I was waiting, and you just ghosted.”

She hesitated.

She could explain.

She could tell him about Typhus. About the panel. About how she almost lost her mind prepping and how it somehow worked. How it felt like the mountain was opening its eye and seeing her.

But instead, she just shook her head.

“I can’t do this right now. I need to think.”

He looked at her, wounded.

“Right,” he said, standing slowly. “Of course. Because this is your thing. I’m just the idiot who tags along.”

“Saiesh—”

“Forget it.”

He grabbed his jacket and headed to the door.

Paused for half a second like he might say something else.

Didn’t.

He left.

The door closed behind him. This time, with a harder sound.

Arenya stood in the stillness of her apartment. Just her, the wall, and the whisper of a stranger’s voice echoing:

Please don’t make me come back.
