# Chapter Four: Firewalls and Favors

Arenya had never broken into anything before. Not unless you counted that one time she climbed through a dorm window freshman year to retrieve her laptop after a party. (She didn’t.)

This was different. This was actual illegal.

This was felony-level illegal.

Which was why she hadn’t wanted to call Saiesh. She knew he’d say yes before she even finished asking. Which made it worse.

He showed up at her door with his laptop, a small black pouch that looked suspiciously like it belonged in a spy movie, and a burrito the size of a small child.

“So,” he said, mouth full, “you wanna break into your company’s internal database, falsify an official investigation, and reroute incoming external data to your private inbox using a stolen clearance badge and spoofed credentials?”

“Yes,” she said.

“Cool. Just making sure I understood.”

She didn’t say thank you. He didn’t need her to.

They worked late into the night. Saiesh ran her through the plan like it was just another debugging session. It was disturbingly seamless.

“Okay,” he said, spinning his laptop toward her. “Teddy uses an RFID keycard, so we clone that first. You’ll need to get close enough to skim it. I’ll walk you through it during work. Then we need a keylogger on your computer—only way we’ll get the actual login.”

“I can’t just guess his password?”

“This is a man who uses an animated corgi gif as his email signature. He probably thinks two-factor authentication means calling your dog twice.”

She sighed. “Great.”

* * *

By the time Monday morning arrived, Arenya felt like she had half of a bank heist in her purse. The keycard skimmer—disguised as a phone case—buzzed softly in her pocket. Her AirPods were in, with Saiesh’s voice in one ear like a morally flexible conscience.

“You got this,” he whispered. “Corgi King won’t know what hit him.”

“Teddy,” she said out loud, stepping into the office, “hey, quick favor—can I shadow you this morning for the back-end publishing process for longform? You said I should learn it.”

He blinked at her, startled by initiative. “Uh, yeah. Sure. Let me grab my smoothie.”

As they walked down the hallway, she brushed past him, keeping her phone angled toward his side pocket.

“Hold it there… okay, cloned,” Saiesh said in her ear. “We have keycard access. Next up: keylogger install.”

Back at her desk, she opened a fake draft of her hamster article and inserted the USB drive into her computer.

A loading bar appeared. One minute. Thirty seconds.

Done.

Then came the hardest part: making Teddy give up his password.

She leaned into his cubicle just before lunch. “Hey, Teddy? That social metrics tool you had me try last week? It’s logged me out. Can you log in again? I want to pull the engagement rates for the hamster piece.”

He gave a beleaguered sigh but stood up. “You know I hate that site. It’s like an Excel sheet had a baby with a root canal.”

“Totally,” she said sweetly, turning her screen to him.

He typed.

Saiesh’s voice lit up instantly. “Got it. Welcome to the kingdom of Teddy.”

* * *

At 5:53 p.m., just as most of the office was trickling out, she made her move.

She used Teddy’s keycard to slip into his office. The door clicked shut behind her.

Inside, it smelled like beef jerky and startup-grade citrus cologne. She sat at his desk, trying not to imagine his aura embedded in the chair.

Saiesh’s screen mirrored hers back at her apartment. “Okay. Log in. Admin credentials working. I’m inside your system too, piggybacking on the IP. Go to Internal Ops > Project Database.”

She clicked through. A long list of active investigations filled the screen: international politics, celebrity scandals, a few social impact stories that Brooke was probably halfway through glamorizing.

“Now click ‘Create New Project.’ Title it something vague but official.”

She typed: Urban Mortality Patterns – SF 2025.

Saiesh whistled. “Oooh. Haunting. I love it.”

“Shut up. What next?”

“Assign lead investigator.”

She paused. Typed: Arenya Sowri.

Her name looked out of place there. Too real. Too bold.

“Route all incoming assets to your company inbox. Add the data share tags. And… done. You’re now officially investigating mysterious deaths in the city—on company record.”

Arenya leaned back and exhaled.

“Footage from the bakery’s security firm should come through within a day or two,” Saiesh said. “They’ll check the credentials, confirm the request, and boom—forward it to you automatically.”

She grinned, adrenaline dancing in her veins. “God, this is insane.”

“Hey,” Saiesh said. “You’re doing what you always said you would. Following the truth.”

She hesitated.

“I didn’t want to involve you in this.”

“You didn’t. I jumped in.”

She logged out of Teddy’s terminal, wiped the keys, and slipped out of the office like she’d never been there. The night air outside was sharp, but for the first time in weeks, she felt completely awake.

* * *

Later that night, lying on her bed, she stared at the ceiling, phone buzzing beside her with another set of pings from the family chat.

She didn’t check them.

All she could think about now was the footage. The answers waiting inside it.

And what Yuri Melnikov had seen in his final moments, frozen there in that bakery.

Something was coming.

And now, it had her full attention.
