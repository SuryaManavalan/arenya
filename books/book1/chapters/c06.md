# Chapter Six: Not Here for the Guac

Arenya had never been so aware of the sound of typing in her life.

Every keystroke in the office felt like a ticking clock. Every Slack ping a potential summons. She’d checked her email ten times in the last hour. Twelve, if you counted her phone.

Nothing.

Her palms were sweating. Not from guilt, exactly—but from anticipation. From the gnawing fear that someone, somewhere, was going to realize she’d broken into her boss’s account and faked a corporate project to receive confidential video files from a third-party vendor.

It was a very specific kind of fear. High-functioning dread with a splash of imposter syndrome.

She kept her head down, staring at the draft of her hamster article. It was already a day late. She’d completely forgotten about it until Marla casually mentioned that “Hamsters are trending again” like they were seasonal fruit.

She hit “Preview” on the article.

Are Hamsters the Next Emotional Support Kings?

(Spoiler: Yes.)

It was bad. She knew it was bad. She didn’t care.

Her phone buzzed.

SF Soul Cycle Warriors

Mira: U coming to Fatima’s??

Fatima: Don’t flake again, babes xoxo

Kavita: Starts at 6! You said you’d come this time!

Oh, no.

She had completely forgotten. Fatima’s rooftop kickback.

The most casual, high-pressure event of the month.

She and Fatima were the kind of friends who posed together for group photos and then made passive-aggressive jokes about each other’s Instagram captions. They had been roommates once. It ended with a silent standoff over the trash can.

Arenya groaned, grabbed her mouse, and blitzed through final edits. The article didn’t need to be good. It just needed to exist.

She hit “Submit to Editor,” closed her tabs, and stuffed her laptop into her bag. No notification from the footage request yet.

She was going to have to be social until it came through.

* * *

Fatima’s apartment was aggressively curated. Everything was beige or pale green or ethically-sourced wood. Soft music pulsed under the low din of clinking glasses and conversations about recent IPOs. Everyone looked like they knew where their lives were going.

Arenya hovered by the guac table, unsure whether she should mingle or disappear entirely.

“Oh my god, Arenya’s here,” Fatima drawled, wineglass in hand. “I was beginning to think you’d been kidnapped by your inbox.”

Arenya forced a smile. “I escaped. The inbox is in critical condition.”

Fatima laughed politely and drifted away before Arenya could formulate a retort. Classic.

She doesn’t even drink. Everything she does is for appearances. It was probably sparkling juice.

Arenya turned—and thank god, there was Saiesh near the sliding doors, sipping a beer and looking just as out of place as she felt.

“You made it,” he said as she joined him. “I thought you were going to ghost.”

“I did. For about a month. Forgot this was tonight.”

“Yeah, Fatima made me do tech support for her sound system so I couldn’t leave.”

She gave him a look. “You really need to learn to say no.”

“Hypothetically, so do you.”

They stood there together, watching the city glow below the rooftop like it didn’t care who felt like a failure tonight.

“Everyone here is so… competent,” she said.

“Mm. It’s the VC-funded lighting. Makes everyone look ten percent more impressive.”

They talked. About nothing. About their friends. About an app someone was launching that used AI to pick the perfect dog breed for your personality type. About Brooke being seen at a political gala with a senator’s nephew.

Arenya found herself laughing once or twice. Not because she felt light—but because pretending to feel normal was the only thing holding her together.

And then her phone buzzed.

[Work Email: New File Received]

She stared at the notification like it had spoken aloud.

Subject line: Incoming Asset for Project: Urban Mortality Patterns

Attached: Video File. Timestamped. From the bakery.

Her pulse slammed against her ribcage.

“I need to go,” she muttered.

Saiesh blinked. “Wait, what?”

“Now. It’s here. The footage.”

She turned, already halfway to the door, throwing a quick wave over her shoulder at a girl she barely knew. “Tell Fatima the guac was great.”

“Wait—are you serious?” Saiesh called after her, already following.

“Yup.”

He caught up in the elevator, slightly breathless. “You can’t just flee a social gathering. There are rules.”

“I break into company networks. I don’t think etiquette is my strong suit.”

Back on the street, the city noise felt louder. Sharper.

Arenya fumbled with her phone, opening the file download, heart pounding.

Behind them, on the rooftop, someone probably asked where she went.

Behind them, someone probably judged her for being weird.

But in front of her—somewhere in the shadows of the bakery’s grainy footage—was the thing that had made Yuri Melnikov freeze.

And she was about to see it.
