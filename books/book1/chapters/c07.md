# Chapter Seven: Fork in the Path

They didn’t speak much on the ride back. Arenya drove fast, knuckles pale on the wheel, while Saiesh kept refreshing the file transfer on her phone like it might disappear mid-download.

When they reached her apartment, she threw her bag down and went straight for the laptop. The screen lit the room in cold blue as she connected to the cloud drive and pulled the file.

2025_02_18_Tartine_FloorCam2.mov

Duration: 5 minutes.

Resolution: 480p.

Audio: none.

She hit play.

The footage was slow, a wide shot of the bakery’s front counter and line. Customers shuffled forward in lazy weekend rhythms. Yuri Melnikov entered the frame at the far left, distinguishable only because she’d memorized his photo. Navy scarf, neatly folded coat over one arm.

He waited. Checked his watch. Took a half step forward.

Another customer left with a bag of pastries.

And then—

Yuri froze.

It was sudden. Jarring. His whole body stiffened like something inside him had seized. His head whipped around—not a glance, but a jerk. He stared hard toward the corner of the café, just off camera. His face—

“Jesus,” Saiesh whispered.

It was a look of horror, but more than that—recognition. His mouth was slightly open, his brow furrowed, like he saw something he couldn’t believe, and couldn’t unsee.

They counted. Twenty-five full seconds. No movement. Not a blink.

Then—he collapsed. Crumpled in an unnatural way, like a puppet with its strings cut.

The video continued, but it didn’t matter. They scrubbed back. Played it again. And again. Trying to catch something—anything—of what he’d seen.

“Corner table,” Arenya muttered. “Ava said four men with laptops.”

She rewound again, paused on the widest frame.

There. Just barely. A sliver of the table in the upper corner of the frame. Most of it blocked by a display case, but—

“Zoom in,” Saiesh said.

“I don’t have anything for this.”

“I do,” he said, already pulling his own laptop out. “Send me the frame. I’ve got a filter enhancement script I used on a wildlife cam project.”

She exported the frame and sent it. They waited in the dark, the soft whir of fans and processors filling the room.

Finally, he turned the screen toward her.

It was still grainy, but clearer. A partial view of two men—one fully visible, the other half-obscured by someone standing.

One wore a light grey jacket, fingers poised above a keyboard. Short-cropped hair. Eyes fixed forward, not at Yuri, but… just past him. Expression unreadable. Calm.

The other was mostly hidden, just a blurred profile and shoulder. But even blurred, there was something unsettling about it. Like the outline didn’t sit quite right. Too… still.

Saiesh printed the image without asking.

They added it to her wall.

The paper curled slightly under the tape. Above it, her other notes. Victim names. Timelines. Phrases underlined three times. And now—two men. Possibly more. Almost anonymous, but suddenly real.

“He saw them,” she said quietly.

“Or something around them,” Saiesh added. “You sure you want to keep going?”

She didn’t answer. Just stared at the photo on the wall, her jaw set.

The hum of the city drifted in through the open window.

She didn’t feel tired. Not anymore.

* * *

Saiesh left a little after midnight.

He didn’t say much on his way out—just gave her a small nod and a long look, like he wanted to say be careful but knew it would just make her stubborn. The door clicked shut behind him with the softest sound, and suddenly the apartment was still.

Too still.

The photo was still pinned to the wall, curling at the corners. The figure in the grey jacket stared out of the grain like he was halfway between real and forgotten.

Arenya sat on the floor, knees hugged to her chest, watching the cursor blink in a blank document on her laptop. A new article draft. She didn’t even know what she was trying to say yet. Maybe nothing.

She’d committed a felony for this. Lied to her boss. Faked a project. Pulled her closest friend into something that could burn them both. And for what? A blurry freeze frame of two men in a café? A handful of unexplained deaths with no confirmed link?

Her head throbbed, not from fatigue but from the tension of holding too much in place. A coil winding tighter and tighter around something she couldn’t name.

This was insane.

She could shut it down now. Delete the footage. Pretend none of it happened. Go back to her hamster articles and her neutral-colored office and being the third most-liked post on the content board. Maybe even ask Teddy for a raise. Smile more. Start dating again.

That’s what everyone else was doing. Building clean, comfortable lives. Choosing ease. Choosing predictability.

And for a second, just a second, she almost believed she could do that too. That she should.

She stood up and crossed the room, meaning to take the photo down. Just tear it off the wall. Put everything back in the box and walk away. Saiesh would forgive her. Probably even be relieved.

Her fingers hovered near the tape.

But she didn’t pull.

Because beneath the fear and guilt and logic… there was something else.

Not certainty. Not righteousness.

Need.

She needed to see this through.

Not for the story. Not even for the truth.

For herself.

Because if she let this go—this pulse, this electric knowing that something was happening beneath the surface—she would never trust her instincts again. Never trust that she was built for anything more than writing cheerful SEO headlines.

She didn’t just want the story. She needed the mystery.

The chase. The danger.

The meaning.

Arenya stepped back. Sat down. Opened the document again.

She didn’t write a word.

But she left the file open.

And she didn’t take the photo off the wall.

She couldn’t. Something in her wouldn’t let her.

Later, after hours of pacing and sitting and restarting the same sentence three different ways, Arenya eventually collapsed into bed fully dressed, not even bothering to turn off the bedside lamp. The city outside her window blinked like it had a secret. The hum of traffic, the occasional siren—all distant, muffled, almost underwater.

Sleep found her not gently, but all at once.

* * *

It began like most of her dreams did these days: completely unhinged.

She was riding a unicycle made of hamsters through an office that kept rearranging itself. Her coworkers were fish in suits, flopping between desks and asking her if she’d submitted the article about “existential hedgehogs.” A giant croissant floated by, wearing sunglasses. Teddy’s head spun like a lazy carousel while he narrated stock market trends in a Shakespearean accent.

In the distance, a hot air balloon shaped like her mother’s disappointed face slowly rose into the clouds.

And then—

The colors bled. The walls dissolved.

The absurdity softened. Shapes lost their cartoon edges. Movement slowed.

And suddenly, Arenya was walking.

She was barefoot, on a path that had formed beneath her—cobbled stones, half-cracked, warm underfoot. The sky above was the color of fading ink. She could hear the echo of her own steps. Each one felt more real than the last.

Around her, symbols drifted into place like props on a stage. A mirror shattered into moths. A laptop cracked open, spilling sand. A journal with its pages nailed shut. The faces of her family, her friends, all behind glass. Saiesh flickered past like a hologram, both smiling and vanishing at once.

The path curved. Twisted.

And then split.

To the right: a water slide. Wide, spiraling, shimmering like summer. It pulsed with warmth. Music and laughter drifted up from below. She could smell sweetness—sunscreen and citrus. At the bottom, she could just make out people lounging in sun-drenched pools, waving her down.

It felt easy. It felt fun. It felt like everything she was supposed to want.

And to the left—

A mountain.

It rose so high it vanished into the clouds, jagged and ancient. The incline was steep enough to make her chest ache just looking at it. The stone glowed faintly red, but the air around it felt cold. There were cracks in the path, sharp turns, parts that looked impassable.

It wasn’t just a mountain—it was something alive. It breathed. Not with sound, but with weight. A heavy consciousness pressing outward. A presence both ancient and sleeping. A question that hadn’t yet been asked.

The slide promised rest.

The mountain promised something else.

She hesitated at the fork. Looked at both.

And somehow—she knew.

The slide would be soft. It would be safe. She could forget the strange deaths, forget the footage, forget that moment of electricity in her bones when she realized something was happening.

The mountain, though—

The mountain would demand everything.

But it would give something back.

She stepped toward it.

The moment her foot touched the incline, she felt the chill bite at her skin. She placed her hand on the rock—rough, warm in places, cold in others. The ground trembled.

Something inside the mountain was waiting for her.

She climbed.

* * *

Arenya woke with her mouth dry and her heart pounding.

The bedside lamp was still on. Her window was open an inch. A crow sat on the fire escape, watching her.

She sat up slowly, the image of the dream still clinging to her mind like fog.

She didn’t believe in symbols.

She didn’t believe in destiny.

But she did believe in decisions.

And hers had been made.
